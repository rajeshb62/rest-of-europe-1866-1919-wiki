# Philippe Pétain (1856–1951)

Philippe Pétain, Marshal of France, was the defender of [[battle-of-verdun-1916|Verdun]] and the French commander-in-chief in the last years of the Great War — the general who restored the French army after the mutinies of 1917 and who commanded under [[marshal-foch]] in the victorious [[hundred-days-offensive-1918]]. Recouly's synthesis, drawing on Pétain's own *La Bataille de Verdun* among its listed sources, presents him as the master of the defensive and of the repair of the army. In this corpus's frame his story is that of the calm, methodical soldier of the old school who held the fortress that became the symbol of French endurance, rebuilt the army when its morale cracked, and then commanded it through the final offensive of the war.

## The defender of Verdun

Pétain's name is inseparable from the [[battle-of-verdun-1916]] of 1916, the longest battle of the war. Recouly counts the German failure before Verdun among the characteristic setbacks of the Central Powers in 1916 — three very hard blows falling on the German staff within a few months, alongside the great Somme offensive and the entry of Romania. It was Pétain who, charged with the defence of the fortress on the Meuse, held the position against the relentless German assaults that sought to bleed the French army white. His method, as the battle's history records, was that of rotation and of the systematic organisation of the defensive line — the method that made "they shall not pass" the motto of the defence. The publication of his own account of the battle, *La Bataille de Verdun*, is listed among the principal memoirs on which Recouly's synthesis rests.

## The restoration of the army, 1917

The gravest trial of Pétain's career followed the failure of the great French offensive of the spring of 1917. Recouly records that, drawing on the experience of three years of war, General Pétain succeeded, thanks to an effort in which firmness and humanity were combined, in restoring the army whose morale had been shaken by the terrible cost of the offensive. He rebuilt the confidence of the troops after the crisis of the spring of 1917, reorganising the front and the reserves and returning the army to a defensive posture that husbanded its strength for the decisive effort. It was this restored army that Foch would wield in the great battles of 1918.

## Command under Foch, 1918

In 1918 Pétain commanded the French armies under the Allied generalissimo [[marshal-foch]]. Recouly shows him in the operations of the final year widening the battle in concert with Foch's plan of multiple attacks: when the Third Army under Humbert attacked, Pétain, *élargissant la bataille vers l'est*, threw the Tenth Army of Mangin into the attack between the Oise and the Aisne, while Foch invited the British commander to enlarge the struggle toward the north. It is the picture of the French commander-in-chief in full cooperation with the supreme command — a marked contrast with the independent fiefs that had marked the Allied conduct of the war before the single command. In the last phase, with Foch, Pétain and Weygand gathered in the councils of the generalissimo, the restoration of the army came to its fruit in the offensive that broke the German army and forced the [[armistice-11-november-1918]].
