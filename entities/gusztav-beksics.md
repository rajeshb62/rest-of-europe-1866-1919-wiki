# Gusztáv Beksics

**Gusztáv Beksics** (1847–1906) was a Hungarian publicist, politician and national thinker of the dualist era, the author of *A dualismus* (1892), the classic Hungarian public-law defence of the 1867 Compromise, and of *A magyar faj terjeszkedése* (1896) on national consolidation. His two works in this corpus present the Hungarian side of the Dual Monarchy from within — the constitutional justification of the Ausgleich and the demographic-national programme of the Magyar ascendancy.

## The defender of the dualism

*A dualismus* was written at the quarter-century mark of the Compromise, at a moment when the system faced challenges from both the Austrian side and the Magyar national movements. Beksics set out the history and public-law meaning of the dualist arrangement: Hungary's independent statehood before and at the time of the Pragmatic Sanction, the common affairs of 1848, the provisional system (the *provisorium*) that preceded the settlement, and the origin of the dualism itself. He defended the common army and the Delegations, the ministry of common affairs and the royal household, and asserted Hungary's international personality and financial and economic independence against the charge that the Compromise had subordinated the Magyar nation. His treatment of Croatia and the nationalities and his discussion of how Act XII of 1867 might be altered mark the fault-lines of the system he championed.

## The national consolidator

The companion volume, *A magyar faj terjeszkedése és nemzeti konszolidácziónk* (2nd ed. 1896), turned from constitutional law to the demographic and social basis of Hungarian strength. Beksics argued that the consolidation of the nation must rest on the free expansion of the Hungarian race: the failure of linguistic assimilation, the alarming demographic phenomena of the Hungarian counties, the relation of agriculture and land tenure to population growth (against Malthus), the agrarian socialism of the *alföld*, and the creation of a Hungarian peasant proprietorship as the means of expansion.

## Place in the corpus

For this knowledge base Beksics is the Hungarian voice of the Dual Monarchy — the constitutional patriot who vindicates the Ausgleich against both the Austrian centralists and the Magyar nationalists. His works stand in explicit dialogue with the German liberal criticism of **Sosnosky**, and together they map the internal strains of Austria-Hungary before 1914.
