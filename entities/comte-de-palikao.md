# Count de Palikao

**Charles Cousin-Montauban, comte de Palikao** (1796–1878), was the French general who headed the government that succeeded **Émile Ollivier's** liberal ministry after the catastrophes of August 1870, presiding over the last days of the **Second Empire** before the revolution of 4 September. His ministry of 9 August 1870 was the regime's desperate final bid for survival, and his name, along with those of **Bazaine** and **Mac-Mahon**, is bound up in the sources with the responsibility for the empire's military ruin.

## The soldier

Palikao had made his reputation as a colonial and expeditionary commander — his earlier service in Algeria and the China expedition of 1860 (from which his title derived) marked him as a soldier of action rather than a parliamentary statesman. When the disasters of Wissembourg, **Wœrth** and Forbach in early August 1870 revealed the army's collapse and the fall of the ministry of 2 January became inevitable, the Empress-regent and the imperial circle turned to a soldier to restore confidence.

## Head of the ministry

On 9 August 1870 Palikao formed the ministry that replaced Ollivier's, with the Emperor stripped of direct command and the new government cast as a ministry of public safety for the war. Ollivier's memoir of the fall of his own government treats Palikao's accession and the accounts the general gave of it with the partisan suspicion of a deposed minister. Palikao's government proved powerless to arrest the German march on **Metz** and **Paris**; within weeks the proclamation of the Republic on 4 September swept it away. He spent the rest of his life abroad, his reputation consumed by the ruin of the empire he had failed to save.

## Place in the corpus

For this knowledge base Palikao is the terminal figure of the liberal Empire — the soldier-politician called in when the parliamentary experiment had failed and the dynasty was fighting for its life, and the last head of government before the fall. His brief ministry marks the transition from the *Empire libéral* of Ollivier to the Republic of the 1870s.
