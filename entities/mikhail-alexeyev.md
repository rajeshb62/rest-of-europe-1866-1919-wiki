# General Mikhail Alexeyev

**Mikhail Vasilyevich Alexeyev** (1857–1918) was the professional chief of the Russian general staff who, in the collapse of 1917, became the quiet organizer of the anti-Bolshevik resistance. **Anton Denikin's** *Essays on the Russian Troubles* treats him as the central staff figure who held the disintegrating army together through the February Revolution and then, after the October seizure of power, laid the foundations of the Volunteer Army on the Don.

## The Stavka under Nicholas II

Alexeyev served as chief of staff of the supreme command (the Stavka) under **Nicholas II** when the Emperor took personal command in 1915. In Denikin's account of the army before the revolution, Alexeyev appears as the responsible professional who understood the fragility of the war-worn army and the fatal effects of politics upon it. He presided at the Stavka during the revolutionary spring of 1917, when the **Provisional Government** and the **Petrograd Soviet** competed for the loyalty of the front.

## From the collapse to the Volunteer Army

After the Kornilov affair and the Bolshevik revolution, Alexeyev made his way to the Don, where he began organizing the first nucleus of what became the Volunteer Army. Denikin describes the awkward partnership — the tactful Alexeyev and the impulsive **Kornilov** — that constituted the leadership of the early White movement, a division of authority that persisted until Kornilov's death in the **Ice March** of spring 1918.

## Place in the corpus

For this knowledge base Alexeyev is the organizational counterweight to Kornilov: where Kornilov supplied the revolutionary gesture and the soldier's daring, Alexeyev supplied the staff discipline and the patient construction of the anti-Bolshevik army. His death of illness in October 1918 removed the one figure who might have bridged the White command's divisions.
