# Alfredo Oriani

**Alfredo Oriani** (1852–1909) was an Italian novelist, historian and political thinker whose sweeping interpretation of Italian history, *La lotta politica in Italia* (1892), is one of the founding syntheses of Italian nationalism. Written at the very end of his life and long neglected, the work became, after his death, a touchstone for the generation that claimed to complete the Risorgimento.

## The loner of Cardello

Oriani lived as a reclusive writer on his small estate of Cardello in Romagna, publishing novels and historical essays that found few readers in his lifetime. His vision was aristocratic, pessimistic and fiercely national: he saw the history of Italy as a long struggle in which the democratic principle had repeatedly failed and only strong national will could give the peninsula its due place. *La lotta politica in Italia*, cast as an "origins of the present struggle," carried this reading from the fall of Rome through the communes, the signorie, the French Revolution and Napoleon, and the Risorgimento.

## The three volumes

The three volumes of the corpus trace a single arc: the municipal federalism of medieval Italy and the age of the signorie; the democratic era from the French Revolution through the Restoration and the Risorgimento proper (Mazzini, 1848–49, Cavour); and finally the unification and the first decades of the Kingdom, from the war of 1859 and the Thousand through the war of 1866, the taking of Rome and the Law of Guarantees, the advent of the Left in 1876, and the African conquest closing on Dogali (1887). Throughout, Oriani's judgment is the historian-polemicist's: the revolution of the South is weighed against the moderation of Piedmont, the democratic ideal against the practical state-building of Cavour and his successors.

## Place in the corpus

For this knowledge base Oriani is the interpretive counterpart to the participant and contemporary narratives — the solitary thinker who turned the events **Garibaldi** and **Tivaroni** describe into a national philosophy. His third volume carries the Italian thread from 1870 through the 1880s with a synthesis that no other source in the corpus attempts.
