# Armando Diaz (1861–1928)

Armando Diaz, born in 1861 at Naples, was the Italian general who succeeded [[luigi-cadorna]] as Chief of Staff after the disaster of [[battle-of-caporetto-1917]] and who carried the Italian army through the defensive victory of the Piave in June 1918 to the offensive of [[battle-of-vittorio-veneto-1918]] in October–November 1918 — the battle that ended the war on the Italian front. Where Cadorna had been the general of the grinding offensive, Diaz was the general of the recovery: appointed on 8 November 1917, in the middle of the retreat to the Piave, he rebuilt the broken army into a defensive instrument, repelled the Austrian attempt to cross the Piave in June 1918, and then launched the final offensive that annihilated the Austro-Hungarian army and drove it from the field. The official account of the battle, published by the Comando Supremo itself in 1918, is the principal source for his crowning victory, and Frescura's *Diario di un imboscato* records the same events from the rearguard.

## The succession after Caporetto

Diaz took command at the darkest hour of the Italian war. Cadorna was dismissed on 8 November 1917, with the army in retreat from the Tagliamento toward the Piave after the Austro-German breakthrough at Caporetto on 24 October. The task that fell to Diaz and his new Chief-of-Staff team was to arrest the retreat, hold the defensive line of the Piave and the Monte Grappa, and rebuild the shaken army — a work of reorganisation, reinforcement and moral restoration that Frescura's chapter "Cadorna" sets against the reformation of the Supreme Command. Diaz chose a defensive strategy for the winter of 1917–18, husbanding the army's strength rather than repeating the costly offensives of his predecessor, and preparing, in the months after the crisis, the "powerful offensive organism" that the official account of the final battle describes.

## The Battle of the Piave (June 1918)

The first test of the new command came in June 1918, when the Austro-Hungarian army launched a great offensive to break the Italian line and settle the war in the south. The attack was arrested, in the official account's words, "nettamente sull'altipiano e quasi nettamente sul Grappa"; where it broke through, on the Montello and the lower Piave, the enemy found itself on the same day attacked and thrown back across the river. The effect of the Italian reaction was such, the account records, that the Austrian official reports lost all hope of revenge — "così ebbero principio sul Piave le nuove sorti della guerra del mondo," the new fortunes of the world war began on the Piave. Frescura's *La battaglia del Piave* (June 1918) treats the same struggle from within the rearguard and the shelters, and the victory proved that the army which Caporetto had seemed to destroy was whole again.

## Vittorio Veneto and the end of the war

In October 1918 Diaz struck. The offensive of [[battle-of-vittorio-veneto-1918]], from 24 October to 4 November 1918, was prepared on the conviction — set out in the Comando Supremo's preface — that the fastest dissolution of the world conflict would be obtained by putting the Austrian army out of action, and so isolating Germany militarily. The Italian army forced the Piave, broke the enemy front, collapsed the Grappa front, and reached the Livenza; then came the pursuit, the recovery of the highlands, the occupation of Trento, and the landing at Trieste. "L'esercito austriaco era così annientato," the official account concludes — the Austrian army was thus annihilated — and Austria sued for the armistice of 3 November 1918, with operations ending on all fronts on 11 November 1918. Frescura's closing chapters, "Dal Piave alle Alpi Giulie" and "Chiuso il libro della guerra," trace the final advance and the close of the war. The victory made Diaz a national hero, a duke and a marshal of Italy; he served as minister of war and died in 1928, remembered as the general of the redemption — the man who, taking command after the greatest disaster in Italian military history, ended the war with a total victory.
