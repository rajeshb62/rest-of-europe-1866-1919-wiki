# Giustino Fortunato (1848–1932)

Giustino Fortunato, born in 1848 at Rionero in the Vulture region of Basilicata, was the voice of the [[southern-question-italy]] in the Italian Parliament — the deputy for the college of Melfi from 1880 to 1909 and later a senator, whose collected speeches make up the two volumes of *Il Mezzogiorno e lo stato italiano. Discorsi politici (1880–1910)* that are the principal sources of this wiki on the South. Where others proclaimed the glories of the new kingdom, Fortunato insisted, in speech after speech, on the backwardness and the burdens of the Mezzogiorno: the tributary system that pressed hardest on the poorest provinces, the neglected credit and the ruined grain banks, the communal domains locked against the peasantry, the malaria and emigration that bled the land, and the colonial adventures that wasted the nation's strength. He was a meridionalist, a liberal, and a pessimist — a deputy who saw his constituency with an economist's exactness and described its misery without consolation, and whose thirty years of parliamentary eloquence defined the Southern Question as a problem of the Italian state itself.

## The deputy for Melfi

Fortunato entered Parliament at the elections for the Fourteenth Legislature in May 1880, standing for the college of Melfi on a program of "dissidence" from the broken majorities of [[agostino-depretis]]. His first published discourse, *La «dissidenza» e le elezioni del 16 maggio 1880*, opens the collection and sets its tone: the appeal to the electors, the criticism of [[trasformismo]], and the insistence that the South's deputies should serve their constituencies rather than the premier. From that beginning he held the college for a generation, returning again and again to the same themes before the electors of Melfi at Potenza, Lavello, and in the Chamber itself. The speeches are dated precisely — 15 June 1880 on the grain banks, 18 October 1880 at Bologna on the credit cooperatives of the South, 2 December 1880 and 19 November 1881 on the communal domains, 21 January 1882 on the exception laws in the tributary system — and each returns the general question of the state to the particular earth of Basilicata.

## The economic condition of the South

The substance of Fortunato's work was economic. He was the parliamentarian of the grain banks (*monti frumentari*) that he sought to transform, of the credit cooperatives that the South lacked and the North possessed, and of the demanial question — the vast communal lands, the "feudal and universal" demanio, which had never been divided among the peasantry and which the state's legislation kept locked. He returned relentlessly to the tributary burden: the *leggi d'eccezione* by which the taxation of the South exceeded both its capacity and its services, so that, as he documented in the appendix to his discourse of 9 October 1898 on *Il dovere politico*, the Mezzogiorno paid to the state more than it received, before and after unification alike. His statistical habit — the appendices on the economy of Basilicata, on the tributary burden, on the state's finances — gives his speeches a documentary weight rare in the oratory of the time, and his own family's position as a southern landowner gave him an authority the demagogues lacked.

## The state, the bank, and the empire

Fortunato's parliamentary career crossed every great controversy of the later decades. He spoke on the finances of the State (25 October 1892), on the war navy (4 May 1893), on the issuing institutes and fiduciary circulation (25 June 1893), and on the banking crisis of the 1890s (10 and 16 May 1895) — the financial collapse that shook the kingdom in the years of Crispi. On the empire he was an early and consistent opponent: after the disaster of [[battle-of-adua-1896]] and the fall of [[francesco-crispi]], he urged the electors of Melfi in March 1897 to support a policy of "absolute recollection" — to confine all military ambition to the Eritrean colony, to withdraw from the interior, and to refuse the "aberration, falsity, and imposture" of the African adventure. He mourned the martyrs of Adua, commemorated the fallen of the fatherland, and opposed the mounting military expenditure (23 March 1901) as a burden the poor provinces could not bear.

## The last years: emigration and malaria

The closing speeches of the collection turn to the two great drains on the South. *L'emigrazione meridionale* (30 June 1909) treats the mass emigration to the Americas not as a moral evil but as an economic fact — "l'emigrazione non è un fatto artificiale, ma una necessità," a natural current of population that only reform could make less destructive — while noting that Sicily, which emigrated little, suffered all the more for staying. And *Malaria e chinino* (30 June 1910), the last discourse in the collection, takes up the fight against the malarial fevers that ravaged the lowlands of the South, the campaign for quinine and drainage that was among the few remedies the state could apply. Fortunato died in 1932, having spent his long life as the conscience of the South in the councils of the nation — a figure whose measured, sorrowful eloquence made the Southern Question a permanent item on the agenda of the Italian state.
