# General Sergei Markov

**Sergei Leonidovich Markov** (1878–1918) was a Russian general of the First World War who became one of the most celebrated field commanders of the early **White movement**, distinguished in **Denikin's** *Essays on the Russian Troubles* for his conduct of the **Ice March** and killed in battle in the summer of 1918. His name lived on in the Markovsky officer regiment of the Volunteer Army.

## In the army before the revolution

Markov appears in Denikin's first volume, on the collapse of authority and the army, as a staff and line officer whose professional vigour stood out against the general demoralization of 1917. He belonged to the circle of commanders who looked to a firm hand to save the front, the constituency from which the Kornilov movement drew its support.

## The Ice March and the Volunteer Army

After the Bolshevik seizure of power Markov joined the Volunteer Army on the Don. Denikin's second volume, on the struggle of General Kornilov, gives him a leading place in the **First Kuban (Ice) March** of spring 1918, where the small White force fought its way through the Kuban steppe against the Red armies. His energy in the marches and rearguard actions became part of the young army's legend. He was mortally wounded in battle in June 1918, and the army honoured him by naming a regiment after him.

## Place in the corpus

For this knowledge base Markov is the representative fighting general of the early Civil War — the soldier whose name, rather than a political programme, carried the Volunteer Army through its most desperate months. He gives a human face to the White movement's first year that the more political figures of Kornilov and Denikin do not.
