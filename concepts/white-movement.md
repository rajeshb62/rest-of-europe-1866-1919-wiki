# The White Movement

The White movement (*Бѣлое движеніе*) is the armed resistance to the Bolsheviks in the Russian Civil War of 1917–20, of which the Volunteer Army (*Добровольческая армія*) of the South, raised by Generals [[mikhail-alexeyev]], [[lavr-kornilov]], and [[anton-denikin]], was the first and archetypal form. Within the frame of this corpus the movement is chronicled by its own commander: Denikin's *Essays on the Russian Troubles* carry it from the collapse of the old army and authority in 1917, through the Kornilov rising and the imprisonment at Bykhov, to the raising of the volunteer host on the Don, the terrible [[ice-march-1918]] of the First Kuban March, and the death of Kornilov at Ekaterinodar. The White movement was the counter-revolution in arms — the attempt of the officer corps and the patriot public to reverse the ruin of the state by force, to save Russia from what the Whites called the "red scourge," and to fight on when the army and the monarchy that had been their world were already gone.

## Definition: counter-revolution in arms

The White movement was born of the failure of the old order and the advent of the Bolsheviks. Its leaders were the officers and generals of the imperial army who could not accept the collapse of the state and the peace at any price of [[october-revolution-1917]]. The term "White" — set against the "Reds" of the Bolsheviks — was not a party or a programme so much as a negative: everything that was not Bolshevism, everything that stood for the defence of Russia against the revolution. The movement was, Denikin insists, no mere restoration: its generals knew that the old autocratic order could not simply be brought back, and they hesitated over every political slogan, torn between the "all-Russian" scale of their task and the local business of holding the South against the Red armies. At its heart was a soldier's patriotism — the faith, as Denikin's first volume puts it, in the old pillars of "faith, tsar, and fatherland," shaken but not extinguished by the catastrophe of 1917.

## From the collapse of the army to Bykhov

The movement's prehistory is the ruin Denikin chronicles in the first volume of his *Essays*. The old army, demoralized by the war and dissolved by the revolution — above all by Order No. 1 and the collapse of discipline — ceased to be an instrument of the state. The February revolution swept away the throne almost without resistance; the Provisional Government proved powerless to command the army or the country; and by the autumn of 1917 the idea of a military dictatorship was already being canvassed among the command, who saw in it the only hope of saving Russia from the soviets. The abortive rising of Kornilov in August 1917 — the "Kornilov affair," in which the Supreme Commander, trusting in the officers and the Allies, moved against the revolutionary government only to be denounced as a mutineer by Kerensky and arrested — was the first open act of the counter-revolution, and Denikin treats Kerensky's "victory" over it as the prelude to Bolshevism. The Bykhov prisoners — Kornilov, Denikin, and their comrades — were the nucleus of the future White command.

## The birth of the Volunteer Army on the Don

With the Bolshevik coup the scattered officers and patriots turned toward the Don, where the Cossack ataman Kaledin still held a refuge. General Alekseev arrived at Novocherkassk on 2 November 1917 and began the "Alekseev organization," the small armed nucleus "destined," as Denikin writes, "to play so considerable a role." Through the winter of 1917–18 the volunteers gathered on the Don — officers, junkers, students, the remnants of the old army's best — under a loose "triumvirate" of Alekseev, Kornilov, and Kaledin, united by the single resolve to fight the Bolsheviks. The tasks of the army were as yet undefined, its means scarce, its political ground uncertain; but the first volunteers had a spiritual quality Denikin honours — men who came to the Don knowing they might die, in the name of Russia. When the Don itself fell and Rostov had to be abandoned before the advancing Red Guard, the army took to the steppe and became a wandering host.

## The First Kuban ("Ice") March

The first campaign of the Volunteer Army, the march from Rostov into the Kuban in the early months of 1918, is the epic that gives the movement its legend. Leaving Rostov in February, the small army — a few thousand men against the surrounding Bolshevik forces — marched south in the winter mud and cold, fighting every step of the way. Denikin records the cruelty of the retreat from the Don — the plunder, the murder of the wounded, the fear for families — and the courage that answered it. The crossing of the Laba, with the junkers wading through the icy water; the running battles at Lezhanka, at Ust-Laba, at Filippovsky; and above all the fight of 15 March at Novo-Dmitrievskaya, the "Ice March" itself, the glory of General Markov's officer regiment. The army turned toward Ekaterinodar, joined by the Kuban volunteers, and laid siege to the city — and there, on 31 March 1918, a shell struck down Kornilov. The death of its founder nearly broke the movement. Denikin, called by the dying man's staff, assumed the command; the army lifted the siege and marched back through the Kuban to the Don, its strength spent but its legend made.

## Current state: the legend of the first rising

In the frame of this corpus the White movement appears at its origin and its purest form: the small army of the First Kuban March, not yet the mass fronts of the later Civil War that Denikin's later volumes (not in this wiki) would chronicle. The movement that the *Essays* portray is the counter-revolution in its moment of extremity — a handful of men who rose "first in defence of Russia against the red scourge," in Denikin's closing words, and whose struggle outlived them by two and a half years before the scattering of the survivors across the world. It stands in the corpus as the armed opposite of the [[october-revolution-1917]], the loyalist counterpart to the religious and monarchist devotion of the [[autocracy-russia]] (though its generals could not simply restore it), and the last defence of the [[duma-constitutionalism]] and old imperial order that 1917 had destroyed. Related to it stand [[lavr-kornilov]] and [[anton-denikin]], its founders; the [[ice-march-1918]] that made its legend; and the [[february-revolution-1917]] and [[october-revolution-1917]] against which it fought.
