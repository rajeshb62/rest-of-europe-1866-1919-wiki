# Trasformismo

*Trasformismo* — literally "transformism" — was the practice of Italian parliamentary politics in the decades after unification by which the prime minister absorbed deputies of the opposition into his government majority, dissolving the distinction between government and opposition and reducing the parties to a shifting mass gathered around the premier. Its master was [[agostino-depretis]], who led the Left to power in 1876 and then governed by attracting to his side the moderates and conservatives of the defeated Right; and its name was taken from a famous jibe that Depretis had "transformed" or "converted" his opponents — that the fierce enemies of yesterday became the supporters of today. The practice was at once the lubricant of the young kingdom's constitution and, in the eyes of its critics, the corruption of its parliamentary life; [[giustino-fortunato]], speaking from the South, made the fight against trasformismo one of the recurring themes of his parliamentary career, and the very title of his discourse of October–November 1882, *Il «trasformismo» e le elezioni del 29 ottobre 1882*, marks the moment when the word passed into general use.

## The dissolution of the parties

Trasformismo grew out of the particular structure of the Italian state. The kingdom had been created by an act of annexation and plebiscite, not by the victory of a party; the Right and the Left that divided the Chamber after 1861 were not classes or ideologies but parliamentary groupings formed around the politics of unification, and neither could claim a social base in the regions it governed. When the Left, under Depretis, took office in 1876, it found that it could not rule as a disciplined party of the sort that governed England or France. Instead Depretis governed by concession and absorption: he offered ministries and favours to deputies of the Right, drew them into the government majority, and so made the Chamber a court gathered around himself rather than a contest of principles. Oriani, judging the post-Cavour generation, wrote that Depretis "conserved of Cavour the parliamentary dexterity and the practice of all the portfolios," and that legislative action was reduced to "legislative empiricism" — a continuous improvised innovation by which the state was kept running without any consistent doctrine.

## The elections of 1882 and the politics of the court

The practice reached its fullest expression in the elections of 29 October 1882. In the discourses of that year — *I partiti storici e la XIV Legislatura* (2 September 1882) and *Il «trasformismo» e le elezioni del 29 ottobre 1882* (22 and 7 November 1882) — Fortunato gave the word its canonical definition and denounced the practice as the ruin of the parliamentary regime. Where a healthy constitution rested on the conflict of parties and programmes, trasformismo rested on the personal ascendancy of the premier and on the corruption of individual deputies; the old "historical parties," Fortunato argued, had been levelled into a single undistinguished mass, and the Chamber had lost every fixed line of principle. The reform of the electoral law that enlarged the suffrage, he contended, was being used not to strengthen the representation of the nation but to multiply the deputies who could be bought into the government's majority.

## Trasformismo and the Southern question

For Fortunato the politics of trasformismo had a particular bearing on the South. The southern deputies, elected by small and dependent colleges, were the most vulnerable to absorption into the government majority, and the South's needs — its tributary burden, its credit, its public works — were precisely those most easily sacrificed to a politics of favour and survival. His whole career as deputy for Melfi was a standing protest against the transformation of the Southern representation into a clientele of the premier. The critics of trasformismo saw in it the cause of the kingdom's political stagnation: the absence of real parties meant the absence of real alternatives, and the state drifted from crisis to crisis — the financial scandals, the African disasters of [[battle-of-adua-1896]], the repression of the Left — without ever putting before the country a genuine choice.

## The inheritance of trasformismo

Trasformismo did not end with Depretis. The practice outlived him and became the ordinary method of Italian government, carried to its highest development by Giovanni Giolitti in the early twentieth century, who governed by the same art of absorbing the opposition and holding a shapeless majority together. In this sense trasformismo is the distinctive politics of the post-unification Kingdom, as much a feature of the Italian state as its constitution and its annexations — the way in which a country of incompatible regions and interests, governed without strong parties, held itself together by the personal skill of its premiers. It stands in this corpus as the negative image of the strong state sought by [[francesco-crispi]], the flexible parliamentary method that Crispi's authoritarian temper rejected, and against which the meridionalist criticism of the [[southern-question-italy]] was partly directed.
