# Triple Alliance

The **Triple Alliance** (*Dreibund* / *Triplice*) of 1882 bound Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy in a defensive pact that became the axis of the "assurance-policy" (*Rückversicherungspolitik*) of the Central Powers against France and Russia. In this corpus it is the framework of Habsburg diplomacy from the Congress of Berlin to 1914, and the source of the rivalry with the Triple Entente that shaped the road to the Great War.

## The German and Austrian guarantee

**Sosnosky's** survey of Habsburg politics presents the alliance as the German assurance that freed Austria-Hungary's hand in the Balkans — the sense that behind the Monarchy stood the German Reich, and beyond the German Reich the Italian ally. The annexation crisis of 1908 and the Bosnian question were conducted under this assurance, with Germany's backing (through Tschirschky and Bülow) the decisive element that carried Vienna through the international storm of 1908–09.

## Italy, the weakest partner

Italy's membership was the anomaly: formally allied to Austria-Hungary, Italy nevertheless nursed **irredentist** claims against it and drifted in the 1910s toward the Entente. **Conrad von Hötzendorf's** memoirs, which treat Italy as the potential enemy of the Monarchy, register the alliance's fragility — the military chief who planned against his own ally. The Triple Alliance held through the Libyan war and the Balkan wars, but it was already unravelling when Italy chose neutrality in 1914 and entered the war against the Central Powers in 1915.

## The diplomatic frame of 1914

For this knowledge base the Triple Alliance is one of the two great alliance systems whose mobilisation locked in the July crisis of 1914: the German-Austrian-Italian (in practice German-Austrian) bloc against the Franco-Russian-Entente bloc. It is the institutional hinge between the Eastern Question, the Balkan policy, and the outbreak of the Great War.
