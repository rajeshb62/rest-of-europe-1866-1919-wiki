# Irredentism (Italia irredenta)

Irredentism — from *Italia irredenta*, "unredeemed Italy" — was the nationalist claim of the Italian state and its partisans to the Italian-speaking lands that unification had left under foreign, principally Austrian, rule: the Trentino (Trent and its Alpine valleys), Trieste, and the Istrian and Dalmatian littoral of the eastern Adriatic. Born in the years immediately after 1860–70, when the peninsula was united but its frontiers still ran along the old Austrian boundary, irredentism supplied the emotional and ideological motor of Italian nationalism in the decades before the First World War, and it was one of the forces that carried Italy into the war against Austria-Hungary in 1915. It was a concept of the incomplete nation: the Risorgimento had made the Kingdom of Italy, but it had not redeemed all Italians, and the unredeemed lands — *le terre irredente* — became the permanent, unfinished business of the national idea.

## The unfinished unification

The ground of irredentism was laid by the incompleteness of [[risorgimento|unification]]. The war of 1866, in which Italy fought beside Prussia against Austria, delivered the Veneto to the kingdom — but by a diplomatic gift, the *cadeau du Veneto*, transmitted through Napoleon III after Prussia's victories, rather than by an Italian military conquest. Tivaroni records the ironies and disappointments of that settlement: the attempt to include the Trentino in the treaty failed because Bismarck refused, and even the victorious general Garibaldi, who had pressed into the Trentino with his volunteers toward Riva and Trento, was held back from Trento itself. The frontier drawn in 1866 thus left the Italians of the Trentino, of Trieste and of Istria under the Austrian crown, across a boundary that ran through Italian-speaking country. For the nationalists this was a scandal and a call to arms; the country had achieved unity but not completion, and the phrase *Italia irredenta* named precisely what was missing.

## The nationalism of the redeemed and unredeemed

Irredentism grew into a political force in the decades after 1870. It drew on the Mazzinian and democratic tradition of the Risorgimento, on the memory of the martyrs, and on the grievance of the boundary fixed at Vienna and then at the Congress of Berlin; and it was sharpened by the policy of the Habsburg monarchy toward its Italian subjects. The Austro-Hungarian state that issued from the [[austro-hungarian-compromise-1867]] treated the Italians of the Trentino and the Littoral as a minority within its other nations, and the irredentist agitation in Italy answered with the claim that those lands belonged to the Italian nation by language and by right. Within Italy the movement was at once patriotic and subversive — a reproach to the governments that had accepted the frontiers of 1866, and a standing threat to the good relations of the kingdom with its great neighbour.

## The war of 1915

Irredentism was a chief instrument of Italy's intervention in the [[italy-ww1-entry-1915|First World War]]. When war broke out in Europe in 1914, the neutral Italy of 1914–15 was courted by both alliances, and the interventionists built their case on the redemption of the unredeemed lands: the war against Austria-Hungary was presented as the fourth war of independence, the completion of the Risorgimento, the deliverance of Trento and Trieste. The soldiers who crossed the frontier on 24 May 1915 carried the irredentist hope onto the Isonzo and the Trentino fronts, and the [[isonzo-battles-1915-17]] were fought in good part for the possession of the Julian marches toward Trieste. Cadorna's plan of operations, aiming at the Austrian boundary and the Adriatic littoral, was in its strategic shape an irredentist design — the drive to reach and redeem Trieste by breaking the Austrian line.

## The redemption of 1918

The victory of [[battle-of-vittorio-veneto-1918]] in October–November 1918 realised, at the war's end, what irredentism had demanded for half a century. The Comando Supremo's official account of the battle closes with the occupation of Trento and the landing at Trieste — the two cities that had been the supreme symbols of *Italia irredenta* — as the Austrian army was annihilated and Austria sued for the armistice. Trento and Trieste, together with the Trentino and the Istrian littoral, passed under Italian sovereignty in the peace that followed. Irredentism thus achieved its object at the very close of this corpus's frame; the movement that had animated Italian nationalism since the frontier of 1866 was consummated in the victory of 1918, even as the settlement of the Italian frontier on the eastern Adriatic would leave new disputes behind it.
