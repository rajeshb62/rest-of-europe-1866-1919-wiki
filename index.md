## The Arc of the Corpus

In 1866 Prussia defeated Austria and expelled it from Germany; by 1871 a German Empire proclaimed at Versailles stood where the German Confederation had been, while Italy, made in the wars of 1859–70, was completing its own unification. The middle decades saw the consolidation of these new states — the Kaiserreich under Bismarck until his fall in 1890, the Third Republic founded in France between the Commune and the constitution of 1875, the Dual Monarchy of Austria-Hungary working out the 1867 Compromise against its nationalities, and Tsarist Russia lurching from the emancipation era through the reaction of Alexander III to the upheaval of 1905. The corpus then follows the alliances and crises of the pre-war generation — the Eastern Question, the Triple Alliance, the annexation of Bosnia and the Balkan wars — into the July crisis of 1914 and the Great War itself, closing with the collapse of the Habsburg, Romanov and Hohenzollern dynasties and the Russian revolution and Civil War. It is built from the memoirs and contemporary histories of the people who made it: Sybel and Lenz on Bismarck; Delord, Ollivier and Hanotaux on France; Garibaldi, Tivaroni, Oriani and Fortunato on Italy; Beksics, Sosnosky, Conrad and Czernin on Austria-Hungary; Kornilov, Witte, Zhevakhov and Denikin on Russia; and Recouly on the war.
