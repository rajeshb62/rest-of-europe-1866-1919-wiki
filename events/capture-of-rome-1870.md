# The Capture of Rome (20 September 1870)

**20 September 1870**

The breach of Porta Pia and the occupation of Rome by the Italian army on 20 September 1870, which completed the territorial unification of Italy and put an end to the temporal power of the Papacy over the city and the Patrimony of St Peter. The event — the fourth and last act of the [[risorgimento]] in the making of the kingdom — dissolved the remaining Papal States into the Kingdom of Italy and made Rome the capital, closing the [[roman-question]] as a territorial problem even as it opened a new one between the Italian state and the Holy See.

## Background

Since 1861 the city of Rome had stood outside the Kingdom of Italy, the seat of the Pontiff and of the remnant of the temporal dominion that the popes had governed for more than a thousand years. The September Convention of 1864 between Italy and the [[napoleon-iii|France of Napoleon III]] had arranged the transfer of the Italian capital from Turin to Florence and provided for the withdrawal of the French garrison that protected Rome; but the same convention, in the compromise of the day, bound Italy not to attack the Papal territory, and France remained the guarantor of the Pope's independence. In the years that followed, as Oriani records in his *La lotta politica in Italia*, the question was for a time resolved within a monarchical frame — the solution that looked to Rome coming by the hand of the monarchy rather than by revolution, as the failure of [[battle-of-mentana-1867|Mentana]] in 1867 had made plain. The kingdom waited on the disappearance of the French protectorate.

## The fall of the French protectorate

The opportunity came with the [[franco-prussian-war-1870-71]]. With the outbreak of war between France and Prussia in July 1870, Napoleon III was obliged to recall the garrison that shielded the Pope, and the French troops guarding Rome were drawn away to the German front. Oriani's chapter on the taking of Rome is framed precisely as *the rivalry of France with Prussia*: the end of the temporal power came about through the defeat of its European guarantor. The [[battle-of-sedan-1870|catastrophe of Sedan]] in September destroyed the imperial government of the French, and with it the last external support of the Papal state. Italy, long the ally of France by the convention, found its hands freed; the monarchy, in Oriani's account, hesitated, reluctant to seize the city that its treaty obligations had pledged to leave untouched, yet the collapse of France removed the obstacle.

## The breach of Porta Pia

The ministry of Giovanni Lanza and Quintino Sella had come into office on 15 December 1869, charged above all with the settlement of the Roman question. In the second half of September 1870 the Italian army moved upon Rome. On 20 September the artillery opened fire on the walls at the Porta Pia, and the breach was made; Tivaroni's phrase is of the soldiers *battendo sulla breccia di Porta Pia* — "beating upon the breach of Porta Pia." The Papal forces, too weak to resist, laid down their arms after a brief bombardment, and the Italian troops entered the city. The temporal power of the Popes, which had lasted over the centuries of the Papal States, came to an end that day. On 2 October 1870 a plebiscite of the Roman population ratified the annexation, and Victor Emmanuel II entered the city to take possession of his capital.

## Consequences

The capture of Rome completed the [[risorgimento]]'s territorial programme: Italy was now one state from the Alps to Sicily, with its ancient capital restored to it. The territorial chapter of the [[roman-question]] was closed. But the settlement of the Pope's position in a city now under Italian sovereignty remained, and within months the government addressed it in the [[law-of-guarantees-1871]]. The Pope, refusing to accept the loss of his temporal dominion, would withhold his recognition and withdraw from public life in protest. In the same autumn, with Rome taken and Italy at peace, [[giuseppe-garibaldi]] — the veteran of the long struggle for the city — turned to the service of the embattled French Republic and led his improvised Army of the Vosges against the Prussians, ending his military career on a foreign field. For Italy, the taking of Rome closed the heroic age of unification and opened the decades of a united kingdom, with the capital fixed at last on the Tiber.
