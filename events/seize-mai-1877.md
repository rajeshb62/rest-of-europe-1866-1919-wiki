# Seize Mai crisis (16 May 1877)

**16 May – 14 October 1877**

The great constitutional crisis of the early Third Republic, begun on 16 May 1877 when Marshal Mac-Mahon dismissed the ministry of [[jules-simon]] and, later, dissolved the republican Chamber. The *Seize Mai* — the sixteenth of May — was the decisive confrontation between the presidential and the parliamentary interpretation of the [[french-constitution-1875]], and it ended with the victory of the republican majority in the elections of 14 October 1877 and the defeat of the Marshal's conception of the presidency.

## Background

The Constitution of 1875 had left ambiguous the respective powers of the president and the Chamber. Marshal [[patrice-mac-mahon]], elected under the [[septennat-1873]] and holding a conservative conception of the presidential office, had watched the elections of 1876 deliver a Chamber solidly republican. Dufaure and then Jules Simon governed as the heads of ministries drawn from that majority; Mac-Mahon came to regard himself as a president who might stand apart from, and even against, the Chamber. Hanotaux treats the coming conflict as the constitutional corollary of the septennat compromise — a foreseen act of authority rather than a mere whim.

## The letter of 16 May

The occasion was the religious question. The republican Chamber, with [[leon-gambetta]] thundering *« Le cléricalisme, voilà l'ennemi ! »* ("Clericalism — there is the enemy!"), pressed the ultramontane question upon the ministry. Jules Simon, a liberal who yet depended on the republican majority, was unable to resist the Chamber's anticlerical temper. On 16 May 1877 Mac-Mahon wrote to dismiss him, charging that Simon could not govern with the president's confidence. The letter of 16 May was the act of a president asserting his power to choose and dismiss the ministry — a deliberate challenge to the parliamentary regime.

## Dissolution and the 363

After the dismissal, the Marshal called the duc de Broglie to form a ministry of resistance and, on the advice of the Senate, dissolved the Chamber. The republican deputies, united against the Marshal's attempt, became the "363" — the body that refused to accept the dissolution and rallied the country. The campaign of the summer of 1877 was fought with ferocity: official candidature, administrative pressure, prosecutions of Gambetta, the clerical question inflamed by the Vatican, and the Marshal's own tour of the provinces. Hanotaux records the death of Thiers during the crisis and the leadership of [[jules-grevy]] over the republican party. The Marshal's manifesto of 19 September and his challenge to the country set the terms: the nation itself would decide.

## The elections of 14 October

The elections of 14 October 1877 decided the crisis. The government was beaten: the country returned a republican Chamber, confirming the vote of 1876. The Marshal's ministry remained briefly, but the Chamber refused the budget and pursued an inquiry into the Seize Mai; the Broglie ministry fell, the interlude of Rochebouët could not govern, and Mac-Mahon was forced to recall Dufaure. The presidential attempt had failed before the national movement that had already imposed itself on the National Assembly. Hanotaux frames the outcome as the defeat of "the politics of the president."

## Consequences

The Seize Mai settled the nature of the Third Republic. It established that the president could not govern against a parliamentary majority — that the Chamber, not the president, controlled the ministry. Gambetta's challenge, that the Marshal must *se soumettre ou se démettre* ("submit or resign"), was answered in the Marshal's own fate: after the republican victory in the senatorial elections of January 1879, Mac-Mahon resigned, and [[jules-grevy]] succeeded him. The parliamentary interpretation of the [[french-constitution-1875]] triumphed definitively; the president became the ceremonial head of a regime governed by the Chamber. The Seize Mai was the last great assertion of the presidential power in the founding generation — and its failure consecrated the parliamentary Republic.
