# Italy's Entry into the First World War (24 May 1915)

**24 May 1915**

Italy's entry into the First World War on the side of the Entente, announced by the declaration of war of 22 May 1915 and carried across the frontier on the night of 23–24 May, which opened the Italian front against Austria-Hungary along the Isonzo and the frontier of the Trentino. The event ended Italy's long neutrality and transformed the war into the struggle on a fourth front that would last until [[battle-of-vittorio-veneto-1918|Vittorio Veneto]]. In this corpus's frame it is the culminating act of the [[irredentism]] that had stirred Italy since unification — the recovery of the unredeemed lands of the Trentino and the Adriatic coast.

## Background

Italy had entered the war years as a member of the Triple Alliance with Germany and Austria-Hungary, but the alliance had never satisfied Italian ambitions. Conrad, who had commanded at Trieste, had from the first counted it a utopia to rely on Italy as an ally: one had rather, he judged, to be prepared to be deserted by Italy, and in all likelihood attacked, as soon as a favourable opportunity offered. When the Great War broke out in 1914 Italy declared neutrality, watching the balance of the struggle. The [[battle-of-the-marne-1914|Marne]] and the whole turn of the war, in Conrad's retrospective, was the signal for Italy and Rumania to seek the Entente — the moment the overwhelming superiority came to the enemy side. The war was not yet a year old when the neutrality drew to its close.

## The crisis of the country

Cadorna's account of Italy at the outbreak of the European war paints a country in ferment. He dwells on what he called the growing spirit of revolt in Italian society — the decline of religious restraint, the universal talk of rights and the neglect of duties, the open preaching of anti-militarism. And he notes pointedly that among those who made themselves the banner-bearers of this disorder were the very men who provoked Austria with *irredentist demonstrations* — as if they could then hold their own against a great military power, solid in its ancient discipline. The campaign for intervention, that is, was bound up with the demand for the "unredeemed" lands (terre irredente) of the Trentino, Trieste, Istria and Dalmatia, still under Habsburg rule. The diplomacy of the winter of 1914–15 moved toward the Entente, and on 26 April 1915 the Pact of London bound Italy to enter the war on the Allied side, promising in return the Trentino, South Tyrol to the Brenner, Trieste, Görz and Gradisca, Istria, Dalmatia, Valona and the Dodecanese — the very terms that Czernin's appendix prints as the London decisions. The Italian chief of staff, Cadorna, complained that he was not even consulted about the territorial stipulations, which implied grave military questions.

## The entry

Cadorna's plan was that the declaration of war, the order of mobilisation and the crossing of the frontier should be three nearly simultaneous acts, to secure the surprise indispensable to success. So it was done: the war was declared on 22 May 1915; the order of general mobilisation went out at the same time; and in the night of 23–24 May the troops already gathered in the Veneto — an enormous force of about 400,000 men — crossed the frontier and occupied important positions beyond it on nearly the whole front. Had Italy waited for the completion of the mobilisation, Cadorna judged, the enemy would have forestalled her, by invading the territory or at least occupying the exits of the valleys. The surprise was at least in part achieved. Italy had entered the war on the side of the Entente, and the Italian front against Austria-Hungary was opened.

## Consequences

Italy's entry opened the fourth great front of the war and bound Austria-Hungary to a war on yet another frontier. The front along the Isonzo and the Trentino would become the scene of the long and sanguinary [[isonzo-battles-1915-17]], the Austrian counter-offensive of the [[italy-ww1-entry-1915|Strafexpedition]] of 1916, and the catastrophe of [[battle-of-caporetto-1917]] before the final victory of [[battle-of-vittorio-veneto-1918]]. For the Central Powers the entry of Italy and of Rumania, as Conrad insisted, helped to give the Entente the overwhelming superiority that decided the war. For Italy, the war that had begun with the promise of the unredeemed lands would end, after years of terrible cost, with the recovery of Trentino, Trieste and the Adriatic — the fulfilment of [[irredentism]] at the price of the war itself.
