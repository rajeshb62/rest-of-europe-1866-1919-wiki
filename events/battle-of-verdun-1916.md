# The Battle of Verdun (21 February – 18 December 1916)

**21 February – 18 December 1916**

The battle of Verdun, the longest battle of the First World War, in which the German army attacked the fortified region on the Meuse and the French army, under [[philippe-petain]], held the fortress that became the symbol of French endurance. Recouly, writing within the frame of 1916, counts the German failure before Verdun as one of the characteristic setbacks of the Central Powers that year — one of "three very hard blows" falling on the German staff, alongside the great Somme offensive and the entry of Romania. The battle's length and cost bled both armies; its name passed into the language as the type of the war of attrition.

## Background

At the beginning of 1916, the third year of the war, the western front had long since settled into the trenches. [[joseph-joffre]], examining the situation from his headquarters at Chantilly "with robust and clear common sense," confronted the third year of the war on two fronts. The German command, and above all the German chief of staff Falkenhayn, sought a way to break the deadlock by attacking the French army at a point it could not abandon — the fortress of Verdun on the Meuse, held as a matter of national honour. The German calculation was less to take Verdun than to bleed the French army white by forcing it to defend the fortress at any cost. The battle thus opened in February 1916 as a deliberate attempt to bring the war of attrition to its sharpest point.

## The defence

The German offensive opened on 21 February 1916 against the Verdun salient. The French defence fell to [[philippe-petain]], who organised the fortress's defence with the method that made his name — the systematic rotation of divisions, the organisation of the line in depth, and the maintenance of the road by which the defenders were fed and reinforced, the *voie sacrée*. The defence's motto, "they shall not pass," came to sum up the whole resistance. Recouly notes that Pétain later published his own account of the battle, *La Bataille de Verdun*, which stands among the principal sources on which his synthesis draws. The fighting was ferocious and continuous, the fortress and its surrounding heights the scene of repeated German assaults and French counter-attacks through the spring, summer and autumn of 1916.

## The failure of the German plan and its place in 1916

The German attack did not achieve its object. Recouly's judgment on Falkenhayn and on the German failure before Verdun is explicit: the fortress held, the French army was not destroyed, and the attempt to bleed France white recoiled on the Germans themselves. Recouly places the German failure before Verdun among the setbacks that, with the great Somme offensive which subjected the German armies to "a most disquieting attrition," on Ludendorff's own admission, and the entry of Romania into the war on the Allied side, struck the German staff within a few months. The cost of the longest battle of the war was enormous on both sides; by December 1916 the French had won back the ground the Germans had taken, and the front returned more or less to the line from which the battle had begun, with the fortress intact.

## Consequences

Verdun's consequences reached beyond the western front. The attrition of 1916, at Verdun and the Somme, wore down the German army while the Entente gathered new strength; and the very course of the year, in which the German hope of a decisive stroke failed, prepared the strains that would break the German and Austro-Hungarian command in 1917–1918. On the French side, the defence of Verdun made the reputation of [[philippe-petain]] and confirmed the place of the fortress in the national memory. The battle, with the Somme and the Romanian entry, marked for Recouly the moment when the Central Powers' failure of 1916 was such that "yet the disgrace of Joffre and the Russian revolution were to save Germany" — the counterpoint that frames the turn of the war toward the [[battle-of-verdun-1916|revolution of 1917]] and the coming offensives.
