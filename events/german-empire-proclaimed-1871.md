# Proclamation of the German Empire (18 January 1871)

**18 January 1871**

The founding act of the German nation-state: the proclamation of the German Empire in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles, in the midst of the [[siege-of-paris-1870-71]], by which the King of Prussia, Wilhelm I, was acclaimed German Emperor and the unification of Germany under Prussian hegemony was announced to the world. The ceremony, staged at the very seat of the defeated French monarchy, sealed the reversal of European power that the [[franco-prussian-war-1870-71]] had effected — a humiliation and a warning to France in a single act.

## Background

The war of 1870–71, provoked by the [[hohenzollern-candidature-1870]] and the [[ems-dispatch-1870]], had drawn the South German states to Prussia's side against France. The victories of the German armies — [[battle-of-woerth-1870]], [[battle-of-sedan-1870]], the capture of Napoleon III — had made the unification of Germany, long debated in the [[german-question]], a practical achievement of the battlefield. The German princes and free cities rallied to the Prussian king; the terms of the new Empire were settled in the conquered territory, with Paris under siege and France prostrate.

## The ceremony at Versailles

On 18 January 1871 — the anniversary, significantly, of the coronation of the first King of Prussia — the German Empire was proclaimed in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles, the palace of Louis XIV and the emblem of the French monarchy that had once dominated Europe. Hanotaux records the scene as the Germans themselves framed it: the King of Prussia acclaimed German Emperor by the princes, generals, and soldiers of the German armies assembled in the conquered capital's shadow. The choice of place was deliberate — a declaration that the new Germany inherited the European primacy that France had held since the seventeenth century.

## Consequences

The proclamation transformed the European order. Germany, united under Prussian leadership and the crown of Wilhelm I, replaced France as the strongest power of the continent; [[otto-von-bismarck]] became the arbiter of Europe. For France the act was a double wound — the loss of pre-eminence and the spectacle of the German Emperor enthroned at Versailles while Paris starved. It fed the demand for peace under [[adolphe-thiers]] and the resentment that the [[treaty-of-frankfurt-1871]] would soon make permanent in the form of the ceded provinces. The Empire proclaimed at Versailles on 18 January 1871 would, until its own proclamation in reverse in 1919, stand as the symbol of German unity and German power — and as the answer to the French question of who was now first in Europe.
