# Expedition of the Thousand (1860)

In **May 1860** Giuseppe Garibaldi and his roughly one thousand volunteers (*i Mille*) sailed from Genoa and invaded Sicily, conquering the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies and handing the South to the nascent Kingdom of Italy. The event is the hinge of Italian unification and is treated in this corpus by Garibaldi's own *Memorie* and by the contemporary national histories of **Tivaroni** and **Oriani**, which narrate it from Calatafimi to the fall of Gaeta.

## From Quarto to Palermo

The expedition began at Quarto near Genoa on 5–6 May 1860, with Garibaldi defying the Piedmontese government's official disapproval. Landing at Marsala in western Sicily, the Thousand met the Neapolitan army at **Calatafimi** (15 May), where the outnumbered volunteers broke the royal troops and opened the road to Palermo. The capture of Palermo and the subsequent battles of Milazzo and the Volturno extended the conquest across Sicily and onto the mainland.

## The conquest of the South and the fall of Gaeta

The revolutionary momentum carried the Garibaldians through the South; Naples fell almost without resistance as the Bourbon king fled. The campaign ended with the siege of **Gaeta**, where the last Bourbon resistance was crushed by the Piedmontese regular army under **Cialdini** — the fusion of the Garibaldian conquest with the Piedmontese state. The whole operation, in Tivaroni's reading, was the working of the national idea that geography, race, language and custom seemed to indicate.

## Annexation and the meeting with Victor Emmanuel

The South voted for annexation to Piedmont by plebiscite; Garibaldi's famous surrender of his conquest to **Victor Emmanuel II** at the meeting near Teano (October 1860) subordinated the revolutionary conquest to the monarchy. Cavour and the moderates absorbed the Thousand's revolution, converting a republican adventure into the territorial base of the unified Kingdom.

## Place in the corpus

The expedition is the founding revolutionary event of the unified Italy this wiki covers: it created the Southern question's map, made Garibaldi the national hero, and set the pattern — revolution in the streets, annexation by the state — that the Kingdom followed through the war of 1866 and the taking of Rome. Its full 1860 detail sits in the 1800–1865 wiki; here it is the remembered foundation of everything from **Crispi's** Sicilian pride to the **irredentist** and **southern-question** debates that followed.
