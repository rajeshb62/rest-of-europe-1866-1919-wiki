# The Congress of Berlin (1878)

**13 June–13 July 1878**

The Congress of Berlin, which sat in the German capital through the summer of 1878, revised the Treaty of San Stefano imposed by Russia on the defeated Ottoman Empire and redrew the map of the Balkan peninsula. Its most fateful provision for this wiki's story was Article 25, which gave Austria-Hungary the mandate to occupy and administer Bosnia-Herzegovina — the legal foundation of the monarchy's Balkan position and the point of departure for the [[occupation-of-bosnia-1878|occupation of 1878]] and the [[bosnian-annexation-crisis-1908|annexation crisis of 1908]]. Presided over by [[otto-von-bismarck]], the Congress is the hinge of the period's Eastern Question, and its settlement stood as the framework of Balkan politics until the annexation tore it up.

## Background: from Reichstadt to the Congress

The Congress was the outcome of the great crisis that followed the Russo-Turkish war of 1877–78. The Habsburg monarchy, whose Balkan policy had since 1866 been shaped by the chancellor [[gyula-andrassy]], had secured its position beforehand by the agreement with Russia at Reichstadt, which had defined the spheres of influence of the two powers in the Balkan lands, and by the diplomacy at Constantinople that followed. Sosnosky traces the whole road from Reichstadt to the Congress — the Austro-Russian rivalry and understanding, Andrássy's Balkan programme, and the attempts at appeasement — and presents the occupation of Bosnia as the culmination of a policy long prepared.

The monarchy's claim to Bosnia rested on the condition of the provinces themselves. Under Turkish rule, Sosnosky wrote, Bosnia and Herzegovina lay in a state of anarchy, agrarian misery and confessional hatred; the insurrection that had shaken them, the *Sturmboten* (the "portents of the storm"), and the failure of the Turkish administration to hold the country together all strengthened the case that the monarchy, as the power responsible for the peace of the eastern frontier, should take the provinces in hand. Andrássy's Balkan programme envisaged the occupation of Bosnia-Herzegovina as the means by which Austria-Hungary would secure its position and prevent a Russian domination of the peninsula.

## The Berlin mandate

At the Congress the powers revised the Treaty of San Stefano, cutting down the great Bulgaria it had created and rearranging the settlement in favour of Austria-Hungary and the other interested states. The decisive provision for Austria-Hungary was Article 25 of the Berlin treaty, which declared that the provinces of Bosnia and Herzegovina would be occupied and administered by Austria-Hungary. The Austrian negotiators, under Andrássy, also secured the right to hold the Sandjak of Novi Pazar — the territory lying between Serbia and Montenegro, stretching in a south-eastern direction beyond Mitrovitza — where the Ottoman administration would continue but where Austria-Hungary reserved the right to keep garrisons and to occupy the military and commercial roads, so as to guarantee the freedom and security of the routes and the continuance of the new political state of affairs.

The legal character of the mandate was deliberately provisional, as a last-moment exchange at the Congress made plain. On the morning of 13 July 1878, the day the Congress was to be closed after exactly a month's duration, the Turkish representatives appeared before the Austrian delegation and repeated their demand that the sovereignty rights of the Sultan over the provinces not be impaired by the fact of the occupation. Andrássy, placed in an extremely difficult position — to sign was to make a concession of great import, not to sign was to put the whole work of the Congress in question — chose the lesser evil and signed a protocol declaring that the sovereignty rights of His Imperial Majesty the Sultan over Bosnia and Herzegovina were in no way to be affected by the fact of the occupation, that the occupation was to be regarded as provisional, and that a preliminary agreement on the details would be concluded immediately after the Congress. This reservation of Turkish sovereignty was the legal ambiguity that the annexation of 1908 was to dissolve.

## The consequences

The Congress of Berlin reshaped the Eastern Question and fixed the monarchy's course in the Balkans. Austria-Hungary received the mandate that led directly to the military [[occupation-of-bosnia-1878|occupation of Bosnia-Herzegovina]] in the late summer of 1878, undertaken with heavy fighting against the insurgent population. The Dual Alliance with Germany and the "insurance policy" of the monarchy followed in the settlement's wake, as Andrássy and [[otto-von-bismarck|Bismarck]] bound the two empires together against a Russia that resented the truncation of its San Stefano gains. The Berlin settlement also left Austria-Hungary as the power of the eastern frontier, whose right to occupy and administer Bosnia under a Turkish sovereignty that still formally stood would become, thirty years later, the very issue of the [[bosnian-annexation-crisis-1908|annexation crisis]], when [[alois-aehrenthal|Aehrenthal]] tore up Article 25 to end the ambiguity. The Congress thus stands as the origin of both the monarchy's greatest Balkan acquisition and of the crisis that set the course toward the catastrophe of 1914.
