# Founding of the North German Confederation (1867)

**1866–1867; constitution in force from July 1867**

The *Norddeutscher Bund* (North German Confederation) was the federal state which Prussia erected over Germany north of the Main in the year after its victory over Austria in the [[austro-prussian-war-1866]]. It gave a permanent constitutional form to Prussian hegemony in the North, created a federal parliament, and — by binding the northern states into one military, economic and political order — stood as the middle step between the shattered German Confederation and the German Empire proclaimed in 1871.

## Background: the new order after 1866

The war of 1866 had dissolved the old German Confederation and placed the Prussian state, as Sybel's national chronicle presents it, at the head of a reordered Germany. The Peace of Prague, closing the war, excluded Austria from the German body and drew the line of the Main across the middle of the country: to the north lay the states that Prussia proposed to unite under its leadership, to the south the four states — Bavaria, Württemberg, Baden and Hesse-Darmstadt — that for the moment remained outside the new Bund. The northern annexations of 1866 had rounded out the Prussian territory that was to be the weight of the new construction. It was out of this settlement, and on the basis of the [[kleindeutsch-grossdeutsch]] decision taken in the war, that the constitutional work of the Confederation proceeded under [[otto-von-bismarck]].

## The framing of the federal constitution

The constitutional text took shape in the winter of 1866–67. Sybel describes how the original draft, associated with the name of Augustenburg, was reworked under Bismarck's hand to secure the predominance of Prussia. The central organs were two. At the head stood the *Bundespräsidium* (federal presidency), which rested inalienably in the King of Prussia, who was to exercise the office of the Confederation through his chancellor; beside it sat the *Bundesrat* (federal council), composed of the representatives of the several member states, in which the votes were distributed so that the larger members could not outvote Prussia. The whole device was, as Sybel's analysis of the balance between presidency and council makes plain, an instrument for reconciling the formal equality of the German states with the real supremacy of Prussia — the same device which Bismarck had already made familiar in the *Zollverein* and which he now applied to the political federation.

## The constituent Reichstag and the Indemnity Bill

The constitution was submitted to a constituent Reichstag, elected by manhood suffrage across the northern states, which met and debated it in 1867. Sybel records the first session of the North German Reichstag and the debates over the federal constitution, the military budgets and the fiscal basis of the new state. A leading act of the session was the Indemnity Bill, by which the federal parliament regularised the expenditure which the government had incurred before parliamentary sanction. In the same months the Reichstag went on to the legislative work of the new state — the military laws that placed the contingents of the member states under a common federal military system, and the fiscal and legal harmonisation which slowly drew the separate states together. In Sybel's framing this first federal parliament, elected by a suffrage wider than that of most of the member states, was the national organ that gave the Confederation its political life.

## The reform of the Zollverein

Parallel to the political constitution ran the recasting of the *Zollverein* (Customs Union), the economic union of the German states whose treaties the war had thrown open. Sybel's account of the new Zollverein treaties describes the economic integration of the annexed territories and the pressure brought on the southern German states to accept the new terms, linking economic unity with the political unification proceeding in the North. The *Zollbundesrat* (federal customs council) consolidated the union as an instrument of Prussian policy, while the particularist interests of the South resisted the encroachment. This double movement — a political federation above, a customs union beneath — meant that even the southern states that stood outside the Bund were drawn into a tightening German economic order. In 1868 the first *Zollparlament* (customs parliament) brought deputies of the North German Confederation and of the southern states together in one assembly, giving the Reichstag of the Bund a forum that reached across the Main, as Sybel records in the opening of the following volume.

## The military conventions

A final and decisive strand of the new order was military. Secret conventions between Prussia and the southern German states — Baden, Bavaria, Württemberg and Hesse-Darmstadt — placed their armies under Prussian command in time of war. These arrangements, concluded outside the constitutional frame of the Bund, completed the military unity of Germany and were, as the German and French sources alike recognise, the step that would prove decisive when war came in 1870.

## Consequences

The North German Confederation answered the *Norddeutsche Frage* that the war had posed, but it left the German question unfinished. To the South it reached only through the customs union and the military conventions; the southern states remained sovereign, their union with the North still pending. The Confederation thus embodied the Kleindeutsch programme in an intermediate form — a permanent Prussian-led federation of the North, holding the South in economic and military dependence without having absorbed it politically. That incomplete settlement set the terms of the next decade: the [[german-question]] remained open across the Main, the Zollverein and the conventions bound the South ever more tightly, and the European powers — above all [[napoleon-iii]] and the [[second-empire-france]] — watched the new German power with alarm. When the crisis of 1870 came, the machinery of the Bund, its Reichstag and its railways and armies, stood ready to be enlarged into the German Empire, [[german-empire-proclaimed-1871]].
