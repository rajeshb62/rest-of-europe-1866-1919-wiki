# The Battle of Vittorio Veneto (24 October – 4 November 1918)

**24 October – 4 November 1918**

The battle of Vittorio Veneto, the decisive Italian offensive of 24 October to 4 November 1918 that broke the Austro-Hungarian army and ended the war on the Italian front, opening the way for the armistice that ended the whole conflict. Fought under the supreme command of [[armando-diaz]], it reversed the disaster of [[battle-of-caporetto-1917|Caporetto]] and completed the Italian war aims of [[irredentism]] — the recovery of the Trentino, of Trieste and of the Italian lands — a year to the very day after the retreat to the Piave had begun. In the official account of the Italian Comando Supremo it stands as the culmination of the whole Italian war, the revenge for the events of October 1917.

## Background

The arrest of the Italian retreat on the Piave in November 1917 had left the front fixed on the river and on Monte Grappa, and the new supreme command under Armando Diaz had rebuilt and rearmed the army in the months that followed. In June 1918 the Austrians made their last great offensive on the Italian front, the battle of the Piave, and were repulsed with heavy loss; the Italian line held, and from that repulse the initiative passed finally to the Italians. The Comando Supremo's preface sets out the strategic conception: the conviction that the most rapid dissolution of the world conflict would be obtained by putting the Austrian army out of action and so isolating Germany militarily — the concentration of effort against the weaker adversary. The preparation for a decisive offensive, projected and perfected through the summer and autumn of 1918, was now complete.

## The offensive

The offensive programme and strategic plan of the battle, as the official account sets them out, aimed at breaking the Austrian front along the Piave and the Asiago highland. On 24 October 1918 — the first anniversary of the Caporetto attack — the Italian army struck. The preparatory action was followed by the forcing of the Piave, the crossing of the river against the enemy's defence, and the breaking of the enemy front; the collapse of the Grappa front followed, and the Italian forces, pouring through the ruptured line, reached the Livenza and beyond. On the Asiago highland the attack carried the high country that had threatened the Italian flank since the beginning of the war. The Austrian army, which had held the Isonzo and the Carso for three years, broke at last; the pursuit swept the Italians forward in the recovery of the highlands and the advance upon the Trentino.

## Trento, Trieste and the armistice

The pursuit carried the Italian forces to their war aims. The occupation of Trento delivered the Trentino into Italian hands, and a landing at Trieste raised the Italian flag over the Adriatic city that [[irredentism]] had claimed since the first days of the [[italy-ww1-entry-1915|entry into the war]]; the cavalry led the pursuit through the defeated enemy's columns. The Austrian army was annihilated as a fighting force — the official account's phrase is *l'esercito austriaco era così annientato*, "the Austrian army was thus annihilated." With its principal ally out of the war, Germany was forced to ask for an armistice, and on 11 November 1918 operations ceased on all the fronts of the world war. The Italian official account contrasts, pointedly, the Austrian report of October 1917 with the Italian recovery of 1918 — the completion of the revenge, one year after Caporetto.

## Consequences

Vittorio Veneto ended the war on the Italian front and, by knocking Austria-Hungary out of the war, hastened the collapse of the Central Powers and the armistice of 11 November 1918. It completed the territorial programme of Italian unification and of [[irredentism]], delivering the Trentino, Trieste and the Julian lands to Italy — the lands for whose recovery Italy had entered the war in 1915 and had suffered the ordeal of the [[isonzo-battles-1915-17|Isonzo]] and [[battle-of-caporetto-1917|Caporetto]]. The victory, won by the army that Diaz had rebuilt, crowned the Italian war with success and gave the young kingdom a place among the victor powers of the peace; and the Comando Supremo's closing tribute — the "glorious list" honouring the infantry, the artillery, the engineers and the aeronautics — celebrated the authors of a victory that stood, in the Italian memory, as the redemption of the war.
