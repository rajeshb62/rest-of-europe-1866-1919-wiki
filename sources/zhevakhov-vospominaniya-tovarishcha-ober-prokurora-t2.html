Zhevakhov — Memoirs of a Deputy Ober-Procurator of the Holy Synod, Vol. 2

Prince Nikolai Davidovich Zhevakhov (1874–1946) — Воспоминанія. Товарища Оберъ-Прокурора Св. Синода. Томъ II. Novi Sad (Novyi Sad): Russian Press of S. Filonov, 1928.

AuthorPrince N. D. Zhevakhov — former deputy ober-procurator of the Holy Synod (autumn 1916 – February 1917); in Vol. 2 — witness of the South, the civil war, and the first emigration
EditionNovi Sad, 1928 (emigration); pre-reform orthography
IA idzhevahovndvospominanijaoberprokuroras3 (oras3 = second volume of the same work; not “volume 3” of a separate composition)
PeriodMarch 1917 – January 1920 (personal chronicle) + extended essays on Soviet Russia and the Church (into the mid-1920s by newspapers cited)
StructureChapters 1–61 (~27,188 OCR lines): chs. 1–25 — memoir core; chs. 26–61 — publicistics/history (Cheka, famine, Church, autocracy)
Previous volumeVol. 1 (1923) — September 1915 – March 1917

Contents. Continuation after arrest and release: life with his sister, Kiev, the countryside, death of his mother (30 October 1917), siege of Kiev, murder of Metropolitan Vladimir (25 January 1918), arrival of the Germans, Hetman Skoropadsky, “satanists”/Petliurists, the Pokrovsky Monastery, the Skete of the Most Pure; 1919 — Professor Armashevsky, Prince P. V. Zhevakhov, G. A. Shechkov; path Kharkov — Rostov — Pyatigorsk (metochion of the Second Athonite Monastery, Metropolitan Pitirim) — Ekaterinodar (death of Pitirim) — Novorossiysk (January 1920). Second half of the volume — systematic survey of “methods of extermination” (Cheka, famine, moral tortures), condition of the Church (Council, Patriarch Tikhon, persecutions, the Abroad Synod, Metr. Sergius / Metr. Antony), nature of autocracy and “lessons of the revolution.”

Character of the source. The same monarchical and church-mystical line as in Vol. 1; in the second half — open émigré publicistics of the 1920s (including antisemitic constructions of “Jewish” dominion — to be recorded as the author’s position, not as established fact). The memoir core (Kiev 1917–19, Pitirim in the South, the exodus) joins Denikin, Essays on the Troubles calendar, but from a synodal-ecclesiastical and Kievan, not staff, angle.

OCR. Medium/heavy: Arabic chapter numbering relatively stable; Ѣ/Е, І/И; «Юевъ» = Kiev; gaps (ch. 27 barely marked in the stream). Headings reconstructed from the table of contents and body.

Raw text: wiki/raw/zhevakhov-vospominaniya-tovarishcha-ober-prokurora-t2-1917-1920-1928.txt.

III. “Collapse of Russia” and methods of extermination (chs. 26–38)

The preface to Vol. 2 declares the reprinting of “long-known” horrors deliberate: to preserve in the memory of posterity the methods of power which the author identifies with “Bolshevism” and with the repetition of ancient models. Separate material on the European literature of the “Jewish question” he reserves for a possible Vol. III.
