# Conrad von Hötzendorf — *Aus meiner Dienstzeit 1906–1918*, Band III: 1913 und das erste Halbjahr 1914, bis zum Fürstenmord in Sarajevo

Franz Graf Conrad von Hötzendorf (1852–1925), *Aus meiner Dienstzeit 1906–1918*. Dritter Band: *1913 und das erste Halbjahr 1914*. Wien, Berlin, Leipzig, München: Rikola Verlag, 1923. This third volume, the last of the three dedicated to the events before the outbreak of the world war, covers the year 1913 and the first six months of 1914, and ends with Conrad's departure for Sarajevo on 23 June 1914, four days before the assassination of the archduke-heir. The raw text in this wiki is the scanned copy (University of California, Berkeley library; Rikola Verlag 1923), 828 pages, already OCR'd, concatenated in page order.

## Charakteristik der politisch-militärischen Lage (1913)

Conrad states his governing conviction with the greatest plainness: the root of all evil for the Austro-Hungarian monarchy was still, as always, the relation to Serbia and to the Russia standing behind Serbia. At this root the evil had to be seized; everything else was secondary. The ways that had always floated before him were, in the first place, the attainment of a lasting confederal union of Serbia with the Habsburg monarchy in a peaceful manner; but if Serbia declined the union and continued its hostile aspirations directed against the monarchy — and this case was present — then a warlike settlement at the suitable moment. Already in 1906, on his appointment as chief of the general staff, he had pointed to this, and in 1908–09 he had described the moment as come to make clear conditions with Serbia, foreseeing that the chances of this unavoidable settlement must from then on only grow worse. In 1913 he held the last moment to have come to carry out this settlement at all with hope of success; if this moment too were neglected, the monarchy seemed to him to drift without a rudder toward the unavoidable doom that threatened it from Serbia and Russia. In this conviction he did everything to see that this last moment did not pass unused. It remained unused. The policy of the monarchy let itself be taken prisoner by side-issues and distracted from the great goal — to the joy of its enemies.

## 1913

The account of 1913 is given month by month. It opens with the political and military situation from the beginning of the year to the resumption of hostilities against Turkey on 3 February 1913, with the audiences and political conferences of this period, the military agreements between Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy and the fleet agreement, and the military measures down to 2 March. Conrad then treats the period from the resumption of hostilities to the rupture of the Balkan league on 28 May 1913, with the particulars of the events, the documents and the conferences, and the Skutari affair; then the period from the rupture of the Balkan league to the intervention of Rumania on 10 July 1913; then the period from Rumania's intervention to the peace of Bucharest (10 August 1913) and that of Constantinople (29 September 1913). He records his presence at the German imperial manoeuvres at Schweidnitz (6 to 10 September), and the manoeuvres in Bohemia with his rupture with the archduke-heir Franz Ferdinand. The year closes with the events from the peace treaties of Bucharest and Constantinople to the end of the year, the consecration of the memorial of the Battle of the Nations at Leipzig, and a retrospective. In the chapters on the essential military measures abroad and in Austria-Hungary, and the concrete war-preparation work of 1913, he surveys Rumania, Turkey, Bulgaria, Serbia, Montenegro and Russia, and the state of the armed forces of the monarchy.

## Das erste Halbjahr 1914

### Jänner, Feber, März

The first half of 1914 is likewise treated month by month, with a foreword and surveys of Albania, Epirus, Germany, Italy, the concrete war-preparation work, additions on the general situation, the events at home, the development of the Wehrmacht, the training, manoeuvres and generals' conference, and the audiences with the archduke-heir and the Kaiser.

### April, Mai, Juni

For the spring months Conrad treats Rumania, Turkey, Serbia, Montenegro, Germany — including his visit to General Moltke at Karlsbad — Russia, France, England and Albania; the general situation; the development of the Wehrmacht; the meetings of Kaiser Wilhelm II with Archduke Franz Ferdinand; the audience with the Kaiser on 4 June; and the instructional journey, manoeuvres and departure for Sarajevo.

### Übungsreise, Manöver, Abreise nach Sarajevo

The archduke-heir had told Conrad, on the occasion of the army manoeuvres in Bohemia in 1913, of his intention to travel to Bosnia in spring or summer 1914 and on that occasion to attend manoeuvres of the troops of the 15th and 16th corps, to be held by order and under the direction of the chief of the land and army inspector, Feldzeugmeister Potiorek. Conrad asks leave for his plan to lead the generals' journey in Croatia, and fixes his own programme accordingly. He is informed that he is to arrive at Bad Ilidže (near Sarajevo) on 24 June and may leave again on the evening of 27 June. On 8 June he travels to lead the great general-staff journey through Graz, Marburg, Pettau and Nagykanizsa, combining with it the intention of learning the terrain intended for the army manoeuvres of autumn 1914. From 9 to 18 June he leads the great general-staff journey; on 19 June he is again in Vienna; on 23 June he travels with Colonel Metzger and Major Kundmann from Vienna to Sarajevo — four days before the assassination at Sarajevo.

## Schlußwort

With this, Conrad writes, he closes the three first volumes dedicated to the events before the outbreak of the world war. The reader may have found it laborious to work through the many particulars; but if he has undergone this labour, he will have gained a true picture of the situation as it existed in Austria-Hungary when the great struggle for existence broke over the old empire. For the creation of this picture the form of the mosaic seemed to him more appropriate than a carefully painted, sensational canvas; he thought it more stimulating for the reader to draw the conclusions himself from the material laid before him than to accept a subjectively coloured summary ready-made.

## Anhang (Anlage 1 bis 10)

The appendix contains ten documents and maps: sketch A of the situation on the Balkan theatre of war in January 1913 and sketch B of July 1913 (as supplements in a pocket at the end of the volume), the prospect of an Austro-Russian war and Rumania, the changes in the relative strength of the armed forces in consequence of the projected increases (March 1913), the report on the observations at the manoeuvres in Germany in 1913, the protocol of the council of ministers for common affairs of 3 October 1913, the verbal note to the Serbian government of 17 October 1913, political and military news from Rumania, the letter of the Italian chief of the general staff Lieutenant-General Pollio of 28 November 1913, and the further supplements.
