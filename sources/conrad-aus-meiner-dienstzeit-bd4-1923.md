# Conrad von Hötzendorf — *Aus meiner Dienstzeit 1906–1918*, Band IV: 24. Juni 1914 bis 30. September 1914

Franz Graf Conrad von Hötzendorf (1852–1925), *Aus meiner Dienstzeit 1906–1918*. Vierter Band: *24. Juni 1914 bis 30. September 1914*. Wien, Berlin, Leipzig, München: Rikola Verlag, 1923. This volume carries the account from Conrad's arrival at Ilidže near Sarajevo on 24 June 1914, through the assassination, the outbreak of war, the declaration against Serbia and the deployment and first operations against Serbia and Russia, down to 30 September 1914. The raw text in this wiki is the scanned copy (University of Michigan library; Rikola Verlag 1923), 970 pages, already OCR'd, concatenated in page order.

## Vorwort

Conrad observes that although this fourth volume embraces only the time from 24 June to 30 September 1914, it nevertheless contains the history of the war from its beginning to its decision; for this decision lay in the failure of the plan agreed between Germany and Austria-Hungary for the decisive first action. What followed was only a stubborn struggle in the endeavour to make good this decision and its consequences. It failed because of the superiority of the enemies, the failure of the means, and the social, or rather national, disintegration within. The defeat on the Marne was the signal for Italy, Rumania and the other opponents of Germany and Austria-Hungary to seek to join the Entente, and thereby an overwhelming superiority came to the enemy side.

## Erster Teil — Der Fürstenmord in Sarajevo, der Kriegsausbruch und die politischen Vorgänge bis zum Abgehen des Armeeoberkommandos auf den Kriegsschauplatz

The first part opens with the assassination of the archduke-heir at Sarajevo on 28 June 1914. Band III had closed with Conrad's departure from Vienna on 23 June for Bosnia, to the manoeuvres between the 15th and 16th corps. He records the political events up to the outbreak of war, the declaration of war against Serbia on 25 July 1914, and the political events from the outbreak of the war against Serbia to the departure of the army high command for the Russian theatre of war on 16 August 1914, closing the part with a retrospect.

## Zweiter Teil — Die Anfangsoperationen in Serbien und Rußland bis zum 15. August 1914

The second part, on the opening operations in Serbia and Russia down to 15 August 1914, opens with the chapters "Kriegskritiker und Propheten", "Die k. u. k. Wehrmacht", "Das k. u. k. (erste) Armeeoberkommando", "Befehls- und Machtbereich des Armeeoberkommandos", "Das Verhältnis zu den Verbündeten", "Gemeinsamer Oberbefehl", and "Wesen der Kriegführung — Kriegsausgang — Geschichtsschreibung". Conrad then treats the military events from 23 to 31 July 1914, the general questions concerning the war case against Russia, the changes in the originally fixed deployment against Russia, the summarized data on the deployment against Russia and Serbia-Montenegro, and the deployment against Serbia and against Russia, with a retrospect. He records the military events from 1 to 15 August (to the departure of the army high command from Vienna for the Russian theatre of war), the extension of the Danube line as a fortified central zone, a summary of the events on the Balkan theatre of war from 12 to 15 August, the general picture of the situation on 15 August, his farewell audience with Kaiser Franz Joseph on 15 August, and the departure of the army high command for the Russian theatre of war.

## Dritter Teil — Der Kriegsverlauf vom 16. August bis 30. September 1914 und die politische Entwicklung in dieser Zeit

The third part treats the course of the war from 16 August to 30 September 1914 and the political development of this time, in a day-by-day narrative, with a foreword, a retrospect, and a closing word.

## Schlußwort

Conrad writes that where in this volume, in the description of the war events, he has unreservedly and truthfully cited individual cases of the failure of k. u. k. troops and leaders, the reader should yet keep before his eyes the high measure of valour, endurance and fighting ability that was required to fight the victorious battles of Krasnik and Komarów in the stubborn struggle with a brave, energetically led and finally numerically superior enemy, and to hold out to the utmost in the battle of Lemberg–Rawa Ruska. The weeks-long struggles of this war period set a lasting monument of honour to the heroism of the old k. u. k. army. All participants may remember this with pride, and it is a duty to think with gratitude of those who sank on the battlefields "faithful unto death", not only for the existence of the old empire but also for the protection of the nearer homeland. He hopes that a generation may again flourish for Austrians that accomplishes the like. In the open and unreserved setting forth of the events he saw the most correct and worthy form of meeting the tendentious, spiteful attacks that were levelled at the old k. u. k. army and its leaders by their adversaries; the objective reader will know how to separate light and shade.

## Anhang

The appendix contains 27 documents and the map supplements A to D (9 leaves). The supplements 1a, 1b, 2, 6, 22a, 22b and 22c are bound into this volume; the remaining supplements and the 9 leaves of map supplements lie in a separate pocket.
