# Giuseppe Garibaldi — *Memorie*

Giuseppe Garibaldi (1807–1882), *Memorie. Edizione diplomatica dall'autografo definitivo, a cura di Ernesto Nathan*. Torino: Società Tipografico-Editrice Nazionale (già Roux e Viarengo), 1907. Four hundred numbered copies printed on hand-made paper. The text is the "diplomatic" edition — that is, a faithful reproduction of Garibaldi's own definitive handwritten autograph, without the corrections or modernisations a literary editor might have imposed — issued by the editor Ernesto Nathan (1845–1921), the London-born Mazzinian who was later Mayor of Rome, with a preface dated Rome, 20 May 1907. It is an autobiography in the strict sense: the whole is written in the first person by Garibaldi himself, framed by his own *Prefazione alle mie Memorie* (Caprera, 3 July 1872) and *Revisione alle mie Memorie* (Caprera, 7 December 1871), and closes with a colophon dated Civitavecchia, 15 July 1875. This wiki's text is the Project Gutenberg eBook edition (EBook no. 56500, released 5 February 2018, produced by Barbara Magni), 203 pages, Italian; the PDF carries a clean embedded text layer, so no re-OCR was required.

Dominant years are **1848–1871**, the revolutionary and unification decades, with the opening chapters reaching back to the author's birth in 1807.

## About the Author

Giuseppe Garibaldi (1807–1882), born at Nice of a Genoese sailor family, was the soldier of the Italian Risorgimento. A follower of Mazzini and the Carbonarist movement from his youth, he fled into exile after the failure of the Genoese rising of 1834, serving the cause of the Rio-Grandense republic and the defenders of Montevideo against the Argentine dictator Rosas, where he formed the Italian Legion and won the renown that carried him home in 1848. After commanding in the defence of the Roman Republic (1849) and the heroic retreat through the Romagna, he returned to exile in America before settling at Caprera. His greatest feat was the Expedition of the Thousand (1860), by which the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies was conquered for the cause of Italian unity. He was wounded and captured at Aspromonte (1862), defeated at Mentana (1867), and in his last campaign commanded an improvised army of volunteers for the French Republic in the Vosges against the Prussians (1870–71). He died at Caprera in 1882.

## The Editor's Preface (Ernesto Nathan, Rome, 20 May 1907)

Nathan justifies the diplomatic method: the editor's task, where the words of a great man are concerned, is not to alter but to reproduce, since any alteration of a text which has "conquered the right to be remembered by posterity" borders on sacrilege. The preface examines the divergences that arose between Garibaldi and Mazzini — disagreements of judgment over events and the attitude to be assumed, expressed in the Memorie in a "hard and hostile" manner — and the editor's conviction that many of these differences would have been avoided had the two men's relations always been direct and personal, untroubled by intermediaries who coloured the one's thought with the other's. The same applies to the relations between Garibaldi and Victor Emmanuel II, clouded by "politics and petty politics," by old-style generals and by statesmen too devoted to the Piedmontese kingdom to embrace all Italy. Whether the outcome would have differed is, Nathan writes, a task for the impartial historian, not for the editor — Rome, 20 May 1907.

## Prefazione alle mie Memorie (Caprera, 3 July 1872)

Garibaldi's own preface: "a stormy life, made up of good and evil." He declares himself a hater of tyranny and falsehood, a republican who nevertheless believes a temporary and honest dictatorship necessary at the head of nations like France, Spain and Italy which are "victims of the most pernicious Byzantinism"; tolerant and not exclusive, he would not impose his republicanism by force. Everything he has narrated, he says, may serve history, "of the greater part of the facts I was an ocular witness." He has been liberal in praise of the dead who fell on the battlefields of liberty, sparing in praise of the living, and ever attacked "the priesthood" most particularly, seeing in it the prop of every despotism — a theme that runs through the whole work.

## Revisione alle mie Memorie (Caprera, 7 December 1871)

The revision opens with the account of his parents: his father, a sailor like his own father before him, who kept his son from the sea until nearly fifteen; and his mother, whose tenderness he honours as the source of the little good in him. He argues that a sailor must begin his career very young, "if possible before the age of eight," the system of the Genoese and above all the English.

## Contents

The Memorie are divided into five periods, each opening its own chapter-numbering.

### Primo Periodo — Youth, Voyages and South America

**Capitoli I–XLVIII.** The opening chapters set the man in his family and his native town, then carry him across the Atlantic: his early voyages and his service in the Sardinian navy; the conversion to the Italian national idea; his flight into exile and the years of wandering. The main body of the first period is South American: his service with the Rio-Grandense republic in its war of independence against the Brazilian Empire — the corsair voyages, the building and arming of the little fleet, the defence of the Rio Grande, the campaigns with the gaucho republicans, his meeting with his future wife Anita; the disaster on the shores of Santa Catarina, the shipwreck, the flight across the Sierra; then the passage to Uruguay and the defence of Montevideo against the siege of Oribe and Rosas, where he formed the Italian Legion and won a soldier's fame. The period closes with the decision to leave the Plata and carry his sword to Italy, and the departure of sixty-three volunteers on 15 April 1848.

### Secondo Periodo — The Return to Italy and the Roman Republic

**Capitoli I–XII.** The voyage home and the offer of his and the Legion's services to the Italian cause; the revolutionary year 1848, the Lombard war against Austria; his entry into Rome and his part in the defence of the Roman Republic of 1849 against the French army of Oudinot; the siege of Rome, the fighting at the gates, the wounding and the heroic retreat with the remnants of his command across central Italy, pursued through the Romagna, until the pursuit died out and the survivors were dispersed. The Secondo Periodo is the narrative of the Roman Republic — the episode from which Garibaldi emerged as the symbol of the Italian revolution.

### Terzo Periodo — The Campaign of Sicily and the Expedition of the Thousand (1860)

**Capitoli I–XVIII.** The famous campaign of May–October 1860. Garibaldi opens with a hymn to Sicily — "genitrice degli Archimedi," the land of the Sicilian Vespers and of the two lads of Syracuse — the island that was to "wake the sleepers." The account runs from the embarkation at Quarto and the landing at Marsala, through the victory at Calatafimi, the taking of Palermo (27 May 1860) and the reduction of the island, to the crossing of the Straits of Messina, the landing on the Neapolitan mainland, the assault of Reggio, and the advance on Naples; it closes with the decisive victory of the Volturno (1–2 October 1860, Bronzetti at Castel Morrone, the fighting at Caserta Vecchia). The Thousand and the southern volunteers are the heroes of this period, which delivered the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies to the Italian national cause.

### Quarto Periodo — 1860–1870: Aspromonte and Mentana

**Capitoli I–IX.** The years of disillusion and defeat. The campaign of Aspromonte (1862) — the march that Victor Emmanuel's government could not sanction, the encounter with the royal troops in Calabria, Garibaldi's wounding and capture, his imprisonment at the Varignano; his return to Caprera; the third war of independence of 1866 with the advance into the Trentino; and the disastrous campaign of Mentana (3 November 1867), where the attempt to seize Rome for the Republic was crushed at the gates of the Papal city by the Papal troops and the French reinforcements. The period ends with Garibaldi the prisoner and exile of his own island.

### Quinto Periodo — 1870–1871: The Campaign of France

**Capitoli I–IV.** The last campaign. Called to the service of the French Republic after Sedan, Garibaldi commanded the improvised Army of the Vosges against the Prussians: the combats of Lentenay and Autun (21–23 January 1871), the three days' holding of Dijon, and the final retreat, ordered when the enemy masses, freed by the fall of Paris and the armistice, gathered to envelop the small army. Elected a deputy to the National Assembly at Bordeaux, Garibaldi took his seat "with the sole intent of carrying my vote to the unhappy Republic," and, seeing nothing more could be done, returned to Caprera, where he arrived on 16 February 1871. His Army of the Vosges, composed of too republican elements, was dissolved by the government of Thiers. The narrative is signed at Civitavecchia, 15 July 1875.

## Appendice alle mie Memorie

The book closes with the appendix giving the full table of contents of the Memorie in its five periods, from the first chapters of the youth and South-American years through the return to Italy, the Sicilian and Neapolitan campaign, the years 1860–1870, and the campaign of France.

## Series note

This volume sits at the head of the Italian thread of this wiki. The introduction of the knowledge base promises the unification of Italy as well as of Germany; the sources gathered so far trace the Austrian and German threads of the continental story, while the French thread carries the Second Empire and the Third Republic. Garibaldi's Memorie supply the missing Italian side of the same decades — the revolutionary and military history of the Risorgimento as told by its most famous soldier, in the campaign that ran from Montevideo through Rome, Sicily and Naples to the Vosges. Its emphasis on the war for Italian unity complements the diplomatic and political histories of the European states that surround it in this period; chronologically its weight lies in 1848–1871, and it reaches back beyond the 1866 starting-point of this wiki to give the earlier revolutionary ground of the Italian question.
