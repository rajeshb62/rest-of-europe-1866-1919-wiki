# Sabatier — *L'orientation religieuse de la France actuelle*

Paul Sabatier (1858–1928), *L'orientation religieuse de la France actuelle*. Paris: Librairie Armand Colin, rue de Mézières, 5, 1911 (© 1911 by Max Leclerc and H. Bourrelier, proprietors of Librairie Armand Colin). A volume of the *Bibliothèque du Mouvement social contemporain* (series secretary Charles Rolland), in-18, 3 fr. 50. The raw text in this wiki was taken from the University of California scan (call no. BR846 S=4; UC-NRLF shelfmark YB 71304) via Anna's Archive; the scan already carries a text layer of 364 page-files, here reassembled in numerical order. Sabatier, the Protestant pastor and historian whose *Vie de saint François d'Assise* had earned him the Institut's grand prix décennal Lefèvre-Deumier in 1905, writes here not as an ecclesiastic but as an observer of the "mouvement social contemporain" — in the words of the series programme, "faire connaître et comprendre le mouvement social, exposer les faits et les interpréter, sans jamais se substituer à eux."

Dominant years in the text are **1870–1911**.

## Introduction — Dans quel esprit est écrit ce livre

The term "orientation religieuse" is not synonymous with "affaires religieuses" — the latter designates what is most exterior in religious and ecclesiastical facts, what they become when carried onto the political markets and used in the quarrels of parties, a chronicle written day by day and left to the newspapers. The book proposes instead to discover "des aspects de réalité et de vie." Sabatier takes up the antique formula *Historia magistra vitæ* and gives it an ampler meaning: history no longer gives only examples of individual conduct, it rises to the conception of the living germs that the past contains and that the present must select as the seeds of a new century. He warns of the infidelity of every historical image and the difficulty of the historian in freeing himself from himself, and dismisses "la légende du Français léger."

## I — Religion et orientation religieuse

A search for a definition of religion that holds for the present time and civilisation. What many simple minds seek in religion is a fundamentally moral foundation. The spontaneous and unconscious symbolisation of dogma. The religious movement always works itself out at the expense of the churches. The Catholic Church and the rights she has to the recognition of France; her activity to conquer the direction of political opinion — the *Bonne Presse*; the uselessness of its efforts. The new religious orientation.

## II — Ce qu'on trouvera dans ce livre, et ce qu'on n'y trouvera pas

The impossibility of being complete. The true significance of the Dreyfus affair. Scientific activity in the Catholic Church; essays of reform and of new religion. Reasons why portraits and proper names find little place here; the unconscious collaborators of the religious movement; certain saints whom it would be dangerous to name; the value of anonymous confidences. The question whether the several churches should receive a place proportional to their numerical importance; conversions; the negative impression produced on some spectators by the missionary zeal of certain converters. The exclusion of eccentric or morbid phenomena.

## III — Les circonstances qui ont déterminé l'orientation religieuse actuelle

The effects of the war of 1870 on the conscience of the country. As individuals the priests showed themselves admirable citizens, but the Church, on the morrow of the disasters, did not know how to rise to the height of its task. The disposition of France before 1870 toward Germany and toward Protestantism; the Prussian conquest dealt an irreparable blow to the prestige of the Reformation.

## IV — Malentendus de plus en plus graves entre l'Église et le peuple

The attitude of Alsace and of France transformed the political question of the annexed lands into a moral question. The inexactness of the formula that patriotism has become the religion of France; the new notion of patriotism, which the Church did not create. The flowering of certain ideas once sown by her seems sometimes to work against her. Among the other factors that strongly influenced the religious orientation must be counted the laic school — accused of being the nursery of crime. The error of the apologists of religion who seek to make of the fear of the "apache" a motive for a return to the faith. The Church, by appealing ceaselessly to the limitless devotion of her children, answers — and answers alone — the most powerful instinct of the human heart.

## V — Les déficits de l'antireligion

The necessity of not confounding free thought with anticlericalism. The impression produced on rural populations by certain manifestations of the latter; a profound antipathy for clericalism found among certain peasants who yet refuse to join anticlerical organisations. Civil and religious funerals. The incapacity of anticlericalism to understand what the Catholicism of the rural populations is. The Church gives the sense of discipline, of solidarity, of harmony. The little success of the essays of scientific religion. Easter.

## VI — La philosophie contemporaine et l'orientation religieuse

It is not the philosophers who created the present religious orientation. The efforts of the national elite, both in the labouring classes and among the intellectuals, to place itself before the living social reality. Christianity at its origins was a popular movement, not provoked by philosophical views. The efforts of MM. Pillon, Renouvier, Secrétan — their high value recognised in intellectual circles — had no appreciable practical influence. Why the present generation attaches itself to the thought of Bergson, Boutroux, William James. The tendencies of the new philosophy.

## VII — Les vues philosophico-religieuses de J.-M. Guyau et d'Émile Boutroux

Guyau and his *Irréligion de l'avenir*, a work of ever-growing importance; his thoughts on a new notion of the love of God, of prayer, of doubt, on what is eternal in religions. Boutroux goes still further in the way of realism, of the comprehension of living truth and the love of institutions; without affirming the absolute perfection of the churches, he sees their effort toward the ideal and manifests to all a sympathy at once disinterested and efficacious, very new among philosophers. His definition of religion; the philosophy of contingency and the historical significance of its success; his thought on the postulates implied by every life conscious of itself, on the results of religious evolution, on rites and the religious future.

## VIII — L'orientation religieuse dans l'art et la littérature

The constant solicitation of our attention by the most superficial facts. Segantini, Bœcklin, Courbet and the *Enterrement à Ornans*; Eugène Carrière and his *Premier voile*, his biography by M. Gabriel Séailles, his ideas on art and its union with life. The little religious influence in France of Russian and Scandinavian literature, and the causes. Sully Prudhomme; Maeterlinck; Charles Péguy.

## IX — Les caractères et la direction du mouvement religieux actuel

Man today has above all the sense of life, and comprehends at once its power and the limits of his freedom. History tends to invade all intellectual domains and even to substitute itself for metaphysics; but the contemporary spirit does not oppose, under pretext of reality, a materialist conception of history to its dogmatic conception — it takes consciousness of life in history, and from then on the guiding thread is found. To whom does the child belong? Neither to the Church, nor to the State, nor to the parents. The historical sentiment in the toast of a village mayor. The lineaments of a laic morality and religion proceeding from the new conception of history. The irremediable weakness of certain religious reforms.

## X — Notre orientation religieuse actuelle est essentiellement française

The unity of France is not above all political; how it affirms itself in the movement under study. Our present religious orientation is not of foreign origin. The mediocre enthusiasm of the country for industrial and commercial successes. The impossibility for France of entering into intimate relations with the German mentality, whether political or theological. Renan, precursor of present tendencies; Dr Harnack; the manifesto of a liberal German pastor proclaiming the piety that may exist among atheists; why "unconscious Christianity" would have no success in France.

## XI — Ses manifestations dans le catholicisme

Two catholicisms coexist in the Church. Her great force is having created the sentiment of unity and of tradition. Students of the Catholic institutes at the lessons of the professors of the State; the prohibition of Rome. The Modernism. The administration of the Church in the hands of a committee of public safety. The success of the new tendencies. The diocese of Milan accused of being a nursery of modernism, as is the University of Fribourg (Switzerland). A page of the R. P. Sertillanges. A note on the *Sillon*.

## XII — Ses manifestations dans le protestantisme

One should rather say the absence of manifestations. The unity of the Protestant character amid its ecclesiastical divisions; individualism. The Separation of Church and State permitted new schisms in the Reformed Church. Why the most evolved Catholics show themselves so severe toward Protestantism. Its situation as attested by the pastors Lafon and Morize. Are the Protestants as misjudged as they believe? Pastor Wagner and the abbé Lemire; Dean Auguste Sabatier. What wounds the sentiments of France in Protestantism. Is Protestant liberty complete? Pastor W. Monod, and how he grasped the religious character that atheism can have.

## XIII — Ses manifestations dans la libre pensée

That free thought is not essentially anti-religious. Free examination according to Séailles and F. Buisson. The significance of the success of the *Cahiers de la Quinzaine*; the École des Hautes Études sociales and its section for the study of religion in its relations with society; the Union of freethinkers and free believers.

## XIV — La création de l'École laïque

If the war of 1870 influenced our school organisation, the soul of the laic school comes from farther and higher — it is the new incarnation of a secular religious effort. In 1882 it was far from being anticlerical. The crusade preached against it by the conservative parties; its results. It is now put on notice to give children the spiritual nourishment they do not find elsewhere.

## XV — L'enseignement moral à l'École

The laic effort toward an efficacious and solidly constituted morality. The moral anarchy from which we suffer. The efforts of Guyau, Pécaut, Wagner; the course of Jules Payot; the essay of M. Delvolvé and his generosity toward religious teaching. Are the hesitations of laic pedagogy the proof of a failure? Laic mysticism.

## XVI — L'École et l'Église

The impossible neutrality. The place and role of the history of religions in education; the new conception of history; the attitude of our contemporaries toward religious questions. The re-possession of the Bible; the return of admiration for the churches and the monuments of the past; a non-Catholic historian at mass at Notre-Dame de Paris.

## Conclusion

To sum up the present religious situation of the country one must note two facts in appearance contradictory: the rapid progress of indifference and an unexpected awakening of religious aspirations — often to be observed in one and the same individual. It seems that we have arrived at an epoch of reconstitution, one might say of religious revenge, somewhat analogous to the origins of Christianity. If the Roman Church appears to have been struck more than any other by the political and intellectual crisis, yet in the midst of the spiritual ruins that surround us present thought goes to seek, to make the foundations and columns of the new temple, ideas and sentiments of undoubted Catholic origin: the sentiment of the mystery that envelops and grips us, of the unity and solidarity of all existences through time as through space; the great law of tradition; the certitude that man has a duty and that this duty is to give his harmonious note in the eternal concert; the conviction that every effort is worth only in the measure that it is disinterested and that the most living man is he who gives, forgets, sacrifices himself. All these sentiments are those of the present generation, and it is the Church that sowed their seeds in its heart. The Roman Church, despite its failures and appearances, keeps in the heart and conscience of the elite of the coming generation a unique, incomparable place, because alone it has taken consciousness of the unity and eternity of its life.
