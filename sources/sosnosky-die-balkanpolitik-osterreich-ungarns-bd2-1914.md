# Sosnosky — *Die Balkanpolitik Österreich-Ungarns seit 1866*, Band II

Theodor von Sosnosky (1866–1930), *Die Balkanpolitik Österreich-Ungarns seit 1866*. Zweiter Band. Stuttgart und Berlin: Deutsche Verlags-Anstalt, 1914. With one map. A contemporary account of some four hundred and thirty pages, drawn from the scanned copy (University of Michigan; Google/Anna's Archive scan, oclc 2757943) via the already-OCR'd Fraktur page texts; the raw text in this wiki is that scan (432 pages). In his preface Sosnosky states that he has kept the boundary line drawn in the preface of the first band and closed the whole work with the Peace of Bukarest (August 1913); he resisted the temptation to carry the Balkan events down to the immediate present, above all for reasons of space and time, given the enormous wealth of the material. He also records his special attention to the relations of the monarchy to Italy in so far as they concern Balkan policy, depicted, as he says, without embellishment, as they actually are, not in the artificial limelight in which the leading daily press likes to show them.

Dominant years in the text are **1878–1913**, the decisive terms being the Bulgarian crisis of 1885–1888, the annexation crisis of 1908–1909, and the Balkan Wars of 1912–1913.

## Vorwort

Sosnosky explains that this band too needs a preface: he has held to the boundary drawn in the first volume and closes the work with the Peace of Bukarest (August 1913), resisting the temptation to bring the story down to the immediate present for reasons of space and time — the material is overwhelming. He further notes his special attention to the monarchy's relations with Italy in their bearing on Balkan policy, drawn as they truly are rather than in the flattering light of the press, and protests that this implies no antipathy toward the Italians.

## Erster Abschnitt: Nachwehen

**Einleitung: Nach dem Berliner Kongresse** — the condition of the Balkan peninsula and of the monarchy's policy in the years immediately after the Congress of Berlin. **I. Andrássy als Angeklagter** — Andrássy under attack at home for the policy that had led to the occupation. **II. Novibazar** — the Sandjak of Novi Pazar, its garrison and its significance for the Austro-Hungarian position. **III. Der Aufstand vom Jahre 1882** — the rising of 1882 in the annexed lands.

## Zweiter Abschnitt: Assekuranz-Politik

The "insurance policy" of the monarchy in its alliances: **I. Österreich-Ungarn und das Deutsche Reich** — the Dual Alliance with Germany; **II. Italien auf dem Scheidewege** — Italy at the crossroads, the making of the Triple Alliance; **III. Österreich-Ungarn und Rußland** — the Austro-Russian entente of the Dreikaiserbund and its limits.

## Dritter Abschnitt: Die bulgarische Frage

The Bulgarian question, the great trial of the monarchy's Eastern policy under the chancellorship of Gustav Kálnoky: **I. Der Staatsstreich** — the coup in Eastern Rumelia (1885); **II. Bulgarien und Rußland** — the estrangement of Bulgaria from Russia; **III. Der Serbisch-Bulgarische Krieg** — the Serbo-Bulgarian War of 1885; **IV. Rußlands Rache** — Russia's revenge; **V. Die Balkanpolitik des Grafen Kálnoky** — Kálnoky's Balkan policy; **VI. Kálnoky und Bismarck** — Kálnoky and Bismarck; **VII. Die Erneuerung des Dreibunds** — the renewal of the Triple Alliance; **VIII. Um den bulgarischen Thron** — the contest over the Bulgarian throne; **IX. Bismarck und Rußland** — Bismarck and Russia; **X. Der deutsch-russische Geheimvertrag** — the secret Reinsurance Treaty of 1887; **XI. Bismarcks Rede vom 6. Februar 1888** — Bismarck's great Reichstag speech of 6 February 1888; **XII. Die Lösung der bulgarischen Frage** — the solution of the Bulgarian question.

## Vierter Abschnitt: Wetterleuchten

The "summer lightning" of the new century: **I. Kreta** — Crete and the Greco-Turkish troubles; **II. Mazedonien** — Macedonia and the rising of the komitadjis, the murky ground of Austro-Russian agreement (Mürzsteg); **III. Die Sandschakbahn** — the projected Sandjak railway.

## Fünfter Abschnitt: Die Annexion

The Bosnian annexation crisis of 1908: **I. Der Annexionsakt** — the act of annexation of Bosnia and Herzegovina (October 1908) by Aehrenthal; **II. Das Echo** — the reaction: among the Powers (England, France, Russia, Italy, Germany) and on the Balkan itself (Turkey, Serbia, Montenegro, Bulgaria, Greece); **III. Der Verlauf der Krise** — the course of the crisis, the Serbian clamour and the Austro-Hungarian mobilization, and the settlement; **IV. Der Friedjung-Prozeß** — the Friedjung libel trial (1909) over the forged documents against the South-Slav leaders; **V. Ährenthals Annexionspolitik** — an assessment of Aehrenthal's annexation policy.

## Sechster Abschnitt: Die Balkan-Katastrophe

The Balkan Wars of 1912–1913: **I. Seltsame Bundesgenossen** — the strange allies; **II. Albanien** — the Albanian question and the rising of 1912; **III. Tripolis** — the Italo-Turkish war over Tripoli (1911); **IV. Der Balkanbund** — the formation of the Balkan League of Bulgaria, Serbia, Greece and Montenegro; **V. Der Status quo** — the status-quo diplomacy of Count Berchtold; **VI. "Der Balkan den Balkanvölkern"** — the watchword "the Balkans for the Balkan peoples"; **VII. Die legitimen Interessen Österreich-Ungarns** — the legitimate interests of the monarchy; **VIII. Die Affäre Prochaska** — the Prochaska affair; **IX. Pourparlers** — the negotiations of the Powers; **X. Um Albanien** — the struggle over Albania; **XI. Friedensverhandlungen** — the peace negotiations; **XII. "Entspannung"** — the (illusory) relaxation; **XIII. Montenegro und Österreich-Ungarn** — Montenegro's relation to the monarchy; **XIV. Montenegro und Europa** — Montenegro before Europe (the naval demonstration at Scutari, Montenegro and Russia, Nikita's triumph); **XV. Montenegros Rückzug** — Montenegro's retreat; **XVI. Kriegspause** — the pause between the wars (the question of intervention, the road to peace); **XVII. Rumänien und Bulgarien** — Romania and Bulgaria; **XVIII. Die Balkanbündler untereinander** — the Balkan allies among themselves, the seeds of the second war; **XIX. Bulgarien in Not** — Bulgaria in distress; **XX. Der zweite Balkankrieg und die Mächte** — the second Balkan War and the Powers; **XXI. Der Friede von Bukarest** — the Peace of Bukarest (August 1913); **XXII. Finale** — the finale; **XXIII. Die Balkanpolitik des Grafen Berchtold** — Berchtold's Balkan policy (the non-occupation of the Sandjak, the creation of Albania, the policy toward Romania, the Prochaska affair).

## Nachwort: Rückblick und Ausblick

Sosnosky's closing retrospect and prospect: he looks back over the monarchy's Balkan policy from the Congress of Berlin to the Peace of Bukarest and out to the future — the position of Austria-Hungary in the Balkan world, the sources of its interest in the peninsula, and the gathering storm that he discerned on the horizon before Sarajevo. The volume closes with a Personenregister (index of persons).

## Series note

This is the second and concluding band of Sosnosky's two-volume account of the Habsburg monarchy's Balkan policy, carrying the Eastern thread of the 1866–1919 period from the Berlin settlement of 1878 through the Bulgarian crisis, the annexation crisis of 1908, and the Balkan Wars down to the Peace of Bukarest (August 1913) — the immediate prelude, in time and in temper, to the July crisis of 1914. It completes [Band I](sosnosky-die-balkanpolitik-osterreich-ungarns-bd1-1914.md) and stands alongside [Sosnosky's own *Die Politik im Habsburgerreiche*](sosnosky-die-politik-im-habsburgerreiche-1913.md) as the Austrian thread of the wiki's Eastern and Central-European coverage in the years before the First World War.
