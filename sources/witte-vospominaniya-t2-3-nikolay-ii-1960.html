Witte — Memoirs, Vols. 2–3 (1894–1911)

Count S. Yu. Witte (1849–1915) — Воспоминания. Vols. 2 and 3 of the Soviet three-volume set 1960 (Publishing House of Socio-Economic Literature, Moscow). Continuous chapter numbering 19–70 covers the reign of Nicholas II from the death of Alexander III to Kokovtsov’s premiership after the assassination of Stolypin (~1911).

Numbering is continuous with childhood (Vol. 1 ends at ch. 18; this block is chs. 19–70). Orthography is new (Soviet) throughout. 1960 apparatus: commentary, index, appendices (vol. 3 includes Meshchersky; polemic with Guchkov).

Vol. 1 of the same edition (1849–1894, chs. 1–18) is separate: source page.

Contents of the block. Finance and the Far East; spirit monopoly and gold currency; Bezobrazov and the 1903 resignation; Russo-Japanese War and Portsmouth; 9 January; Manifesto of 17 October; half-year premiership; loan of 1906; First and Second Dumas; 3 June 1907; Stolypin; Kokovtsov. Junction with Kornilov, Part 3 and Granat, Vol. 8.

Character of the OCR. Medium: running heads, artifacts (“ПЯТИ”ДЕСЯТАЯ” = fiftieth, “ВЕЛИКИЙ КИЯЗЬ”, “ВЕСПОРЯДКИ”, etc.); headings restored. Chapter 50 in the OCR stream is mistitled but stands correctly between 49 and 51.

Below — the 1960 table of contents with brief anchors from the volume text.

Vol. 2 — Chapters 19–51 (1894 – eve of 17 October 1905)

Chapter 19. Beginning of the reign

Death of Alexander III “as a true Christian”; conveyance of the body; first days of Nicholas II; reports and appointments. (≈ 1923 ch. I.)

Chapter 20. Negotiations with Li Hung-chang and conclusion of the treaty with China

Shimonoseki; loan guarantee; Russo-Chinese Bank; Li at the coronation; Chinese Eastern Railway. (≈ 1923 II.)

Chapter 21. Coronation. Khodynka. Treaty with Japan concerning Korea. Nizhny Novgorod exhibition. The sovereign’s journey

Khodynka catastrophe; Korean agreement; exhibition; European visits. (≈ 1923 III.)

Chapter 22. Spirit monopoly

2 June 1896 — reorganization of the department of non-assessed revenues; state sale of drink. (≈ 1923 IV.)

Chapter 23. Gold currency

Monetary reform laid down still under Alexander III; carried through under Nicholas II. (≈ 1923 V.)

Chapter 24. Project for seizure of the Bosphorus. New policy in the borderlands

Nelidov’s memorandum; conference; changes of viceroys (Caucasus, Warsaw, Finland). (≈ 1923 VI.)

Chapter 25. Appointment of Count Muravyov as minister of foreign affairs. Resignation of Vorontsov-Dashkov

Ministry of Foreign Affairs; Ministry of the Court; Frederiks. (≈ 1923 VII.)

Chapter 26. Arrival in Petersburg in 1897 of Emperor Franz Joseph, Wilhelm II, and the President of the French Republic

Visits of the monarchs and of Félix Faure. (≈ 1923 VIII.)

Chapter 27. Seizure of the Liaodong Peninsula

Urgent despatch at a session of the plague commission (1897); Port Arthur / Dalny. (≈ 1923 IX.)

Chapter 28. A. N. Kuropatkin

Departure of Vannovsky; Kuropatkin as war minister. (≈ 1923 X.)

Chapter 29. The Hague Conference

Initiative of 1898; Muravyov; Witte’s attitude. (≈ 1923 XI.)

Chapter 30. I. L. Goremykin

1899; tour of the monopoly provinces; Crimea; Goremykin at the MVD. (≈ 1923 XII.)

Chapter 31. The Boxer rising and our policy in the Far East

Behind China — Britain and the powers; Petersburg’s line. (≈ 1923 XIII.)

Chapter 32. My journey to Paris for the World Exhibition. Stop in Copenhagen. Illness of the sovereign

1900; exhibition; illness of Nicholas II. (≈ 1923 XIV.)

Chapter 33. Murder of N. P. Bogolepov and of D. S. Sipyagin

14 February 1901 — attempt on Bogolepov; then Sipyagin. (≈ 1923 XV.)

Chapter 34. V. K. Plehve

Personal enmity (“twice prevented” appointment as minister of internal affairs); Plehve’s course. (≈ 1923 XVI.)

Chapter 35. Negotiations with Marquis Ito. My journey to the Far East. Creation of the Chief Administration of Merchant Shipping and Ports

Ito; inspection of the East; new chief administration. (≈ 1923 XVII.)

Chapter 36. Growth of Bezobrazov’s influence. My resignation

1903; Sarov; the “Bezobrazov clique”; departure from the finance ministry / chairmanship of the Committee of Ministers. (≈ 1923 XVIII.)

Chapter 37. My journey to Paris in the autumn of 1903. Characterization of the ruling circles

Post of chairman of the Committee of Ministers; observations on the upper circles. (≈ 1923 XIX.)

Chapter 38. War with Japan

Visit of Kurino; beginning and course of the war in Witte’s account. (≈ 1923 XX.)

Chapter 39. Conclusion of the second commercial treaty with Germany in 1904

After leaving the finance ministry; negotiations with Berlin. (≈ 1923 XXI.)

Chapter 40. Appointment of Svyatopolk-Mirsky as minister of internal affairs. Ukase of 12 December 1904

After Plehve; Svyatopolk-Mirsky’s “spring”; ukase of 12 December. (≈ 1923 XXII.)

Chapter 41. 9 January

6 January — Epiphany shot on the Neva; 9 January 1905. (≈ 1923 XXIII.)

Chapter 42. Work in execution of the ukase of 12 December

Committee of Ministers; measures for the “establishment of legality.” (≈ 1923 XXIV.)

Chapter 43. Manifesto on disorders and troubles and ukase to Bulygin on drawing in the best people

February 1905; Bulygin. (≈ 1923 XXV.)

Chapter 44. Tsushima

Oscillations “to the left” parallel with defeats; Tsushima. (≈ 1923 XXVI.)

Chapter 45. The Portsmouth peace

Roosevelt’s mediation; Witte’s mission to America; terms of peace. (≈ 1923 XXVII.)

Chapter 46. Visit to Paris on the return journey from America

First European port; Paris. (≈ 1923 XXVIII.)

Chapter 47. Rominten

Berlin; embassy warnings; meeting at Rominten. (≈ 1923 XXIX.)

Chapter 48. Arrival in Petersburg

Return; reception. (≈ 1923 XXX.)

Chapter 49. The Bulygin Duma

Discussion of Bulygin’s project already before America; course of the work. (≈ 1923 XXXI.)

Chapter 50. (in OCR: “Fiftieth” — heading damaged)

Transitional chapters on the eve of October 1905 (conferences, memoranda, pressure of events). Between “The Bulygin Duma” and “On the eve of 17 October.” (≈ 1923 XXXII.)

Chapter 51. On the eve of 17 October

Paris conversations (Naryshkina and others); preparation of the manifesto; Witte’s position. End of Vol. 2. (≈ 1923 XXXIII.)

Vol. 3 — Chapters 52–70 (17 October 1905–1911)

Title of Vol. 3: reign of Nicholas II (continued); 17 October 1905 – 1911. At the end of the volume — appendices (Prince V. P. Meshchersky; polemic with A. I. Guchkov), commentary, name index. Print run 75,000; set from 7 April 1960, signed for press 31 August 1960 (in the imprint OCR — confusion of dates “1954”).

Chapter 52. Manifesto of 17 October

Manifesto “On the improvement of the state order”; Witte’s role. (≈ 1923 XXXIV.)

Chapter 53. First days of my premiership

17 October — home to dinner; next day again to Peterhof. (≈ 1923 XXXV.)

Chapter 54. Trepov and Grand Duke Nikolai Nikolaevich

Pressure of Trepov and Grand Duke Nikolai Nikolaevich on the cabinet. (≈ 1923 XXXVI.)

Chapter 55. Formation of the cabinet. Amnesty. Electoral law

Composition of the government; amnesty; electoral law. (≈ 1923 XXXVII.)

Chapter 56. Disorders and punitive expeditions

“Complete … confusion” of the empire; punitive measures. (≈ 1923 XXXVIII.)

Chapter 57. Resignation of Manukhin and of Timiryazev

S. S. Manukhin (justice) → M. G. Akimov; departure of Timiryazev. (≈ 1923 XXXIX.)

Chapter 58. Resignation of Kutler. Intrigues of the right

Peasant question; Kutler; right-wing pressure. (≈ 1923 XL.)

Chapter 59. The loan

Liquidation of war expenditures; large foreign loan of 1906. (≈ 1923 XLI.)

Chapter 60. Finland

Appointment of Gerard as governor-general of Finland; Finnish policy of the premiership. (≈ 1923 XLII.)

Chapter 61. Institution of the State Duma. Fundamental Laws

After 17 October; Fundamental Laws of 1906; Solsky commission. (≈ 1923 XLIII.)

Chapter 62. Principal legislative measures carried in my premiership

Summary of the laws of the half-year cabinet. (≈ 1923 XLIV.)

Chapter 63. My resignation

Assessment of Nicholas II; circumstances of departure (April 1906). (≈ 1923 XLV.)

Chapter 64. The First Duma. Stolypin

“Duma of popular retribution” / of inexperience; appearance of Stolypin. (≈ 1923 XLVI.)

Chapter 65. My journey abroad in the summer of 1906

May and summer 1906; observations from abroad. (≈ 1923 XLVII.)

Chapter 66. Between the First and Second Dumas

Rules of the “inter-Duma” period; preparation for the Second Duma. (≈ 1923 XLVIII.)

Chapter 67. Attempt on my life

Guard at the house; two attempts; warnings. (≈ 1923 XLIX.)

Chapter 68. The Second Duma. State coup of 3 June 1907

Opening of the Second Duma 20 February; dissolution; change of the electoral law 3 June 1907 (“state coup” in Witte’s designation). (≈ 1923 L.)

Chapter 69. From the state coup of 3 June 1907 to the assassination of Stolypin 1 September 1911

The Stolypin regime in Witte’s assessment; frictions at the top; Kiev. (≈ 1923 LI.)

Chapter 70. Kokovtsov — prime minister

After the attempt on Stolypin: Kokovtsov — chairman of the Council; Makarov — MVD; Kryzhanovsky — state secretary; Trusevich and the revision of the Kiev security section; Sazonov, the Union of the Russian People, Germogen, Iliodor, Rasputin; expectations of society. End of the memoir series. (≈ 1923 LII.)

Further in Vol. 3 — appendices, commentary, name index.

Do Vols. 2 + 3 close the whole narrative of Nicholas II in the continuous 1960 numbering?Yes — chs. 19–70 (= 52 chapters), from the death of Alexander III to Kokovtsov ~1911.
Is there a continuation after ch. 70?In this three-volume set no (thereafter — apparatus).
Are Witte’s Memoirs complete as the 1960 three-volume set together with Vol. 1?Yes — Vol. 1 (chs. 1–18, 1849–1894) + Vols. 2–3 (19–70).
