# Attilio Frescura — *Diario di un imboscato*

Attilio Frescura (1887–1943), *Diario di un imboscato*, Galla Editore, Vicenza, 1919. The title page bears the publisher's device and an epigraph from Jean Jaurès. The book is prefaced by the editor, dated Vicenza, 22 August 1919, which insists that the author — whose title seems to brand him a shirker — was in fact a decorated territorial officer who fought from 1915 to 1918, winning a silver and a bronze medal for military valour and three war crosses, and who was at Asiago in 1916, at Caporetto in 1917, and on the Piave in 1918. The text of this wiki is the scan (536 pages, of which 488 carry full text).

The work is a satirical diary of the Italian front told in the first person from the persona of the "imboscato" — the man sheltered from the trenches in the rearguard, the depots, and the staffs. Its frame is set in the opening pages: "Le gradazioni dell'«imboscato» sono infinite. Il combattente ha sempre qualcuno che è «imboscato» rispetto a sè..." (The gradations of the "shirker" are infinite. The combatant always has someone who is a "shirker" relative to himself...). The diary opens with a note that the first sheets, recording the heroic exaltation of the crowd in the days of preparation and the events of the first days of the war, were lost in part when the manuscript was saved in the second half of May 1916.

The diary runs from **24 May 1915** (the first cannon shot from Fort Verena at four in the morning, the crossing of the border) to the closing of the war in **November 1918**.

## Chapters

**I. "I Terribili"** — the opening of the war at Asiago: the first days at Fort Verena and the ridge, the brigades Ivrea and Brescia, the attack of 29 May 1915, the passage of the border, and the disappointed first offensive. Dated entries begin (20 July 1915). **Ottobre 1915** — a section on the second autumn of waiting. **II. "Battute d'aspetto"** — the waiting episodes of the winter of 1915–1916. **"L'offensiva austriaca nell'altopiano di Asiago"** — the Austrian offensive (the Strafexpedition) of May–June 1916 on the Asiago highland, against which the author's manuscript was saved. **Luglio 1916** — the aftermath of the offensive, with the papers of the Supreme Command on the Trentino campaign. **VII. Il Carso** — the fighting on the Carso. **IX. Pace in guerra** — "peace in war," the truces and lulls. **X. Paese di qua e paese di là** — the country on this side and the other. **XI. Pazzi e savi** — madmen and sages. **XII. Smorfie e sberleffi** — grimaces and taunts. **XIII. Il buon giudice** — the good judge. **XIV. Censori e censura** — the censors and the censorship. **XV. Il "siluro"** — "the torpedo." **XVI. Imbasciate** — embassies. **XVII. Caporetto** — the retreat of October–November 1917. **XVIII. Cadorna** — the dismissal of the Chief of Staff and the reorganisation of the Supreme Command. **XIX. Un generale** — a general. **XX. Scaramucce nelle retrovie del Piave** — skirmishes in the rearguard of the Piave. **XXI. La battaglia del Piave** — the battle of the Piave (June 1918). **XXII. Il cattivo giudice** — the bad judge. **XXIII. Dal Piave alle Alpi Giulie** — from the Piave to the Julian Alps, the advance after Vittorio Veneto. **XXIV. Chiuso il libro della guerra** — "Closed the Book of War," the close of the diary at the end of the war, closing with the same Jaurès epigraph with which the volume opened.
