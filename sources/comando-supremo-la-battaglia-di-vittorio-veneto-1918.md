# Regio Esercito Italiano, Comando Supremo — *La battaglia di Vittorio Veneto, 24 ottobre–4 novembre 1918*

*La battaglia di Vittorio Veneto, 24 ottobre – 4 novembre 1918*, published by the Comando Supremo of the Regio Esercito Italiano, 1918 (OCLC 10783357). The text of this wiki is the HathiTrust full-text scan (University of Michigan copy, 60 pages, a gift of the Italian Military Attaché).

The volume is the official account of the final Italian offensive, written at the moment of victory. Its **Premessa** (Preface) sets out the conviction of the Supreme Command that the most rapid dissolution of the world conflict would be obtained by putting the Austrian army out of action and so isolating Germany militarily — the concentration of effort against the weaker adversary, the plan for an action on the Asiago highland in spring 1918, and the preparation, in the months after the crisis of 1917, of a powerful offensive organism.

## Structure

**Parte I. La preparazione** — the preparation: the situation after the enemy offensive of June 1918 (the Battle of the Piave), the offensive programme, the strategic plan of the battle, the dispositions, and the opposing forces. **Parte II. La battaglia** — the battle (24 October – 4 November 1918), in four movements: (1) the preparatory action; (2) the breaking of the enemy front — the forcing of the Piave, the crisis, the delineating success, the collapse of the Grappa front, the reaching of the Livenza, and the action on the Asiago highland; (3) the pursuit — the recovery of the highlands, the occupation of Trento, the last resistances of the enemy, the landing at Trieste, and the cavalry in the pursuit; (4) the armistice — "L'esercito austriaco era così annientato" (The Austrian army was thus annihilated), with Germany forced to ask for an armistice and the end of operations on all fronts on 11 November 1918. The part closes by contrasting the Austrian official report of October 1917 with the Italian recovery and the completion of the revenge a year later. **Parte III. Gli artefici della vittoria** — the authors of the victory: "Un elenco glorioso" (A glorious list), honouring the infantry (the *Fante d'Italia*), the artillery and bombardiers, the engineers and the pontieri of the 4th Regiment, the cavalry in the pursuit, and the aeronautics. **Appendice. Le truppe italiane sugli altri campi della guerra mondiale** — Italian troops on the other fields of the world war: France (the II Corps on the Ardre in July 1918, the Chemin des Dames and the pursuit, the taking of Rozoy-sur-Serre), Albania, Macedonia (the Cerna and the offensive that brought down the Bulgarian front, the taking of Krusevo, the armistice of Salonicco), and Palestine.

The volume ends with an **INDICE** (table of contents) followed by a fold-out map of the theatre.
