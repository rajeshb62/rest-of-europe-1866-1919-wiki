# Sosnosky — *Die Politik im Habsburgerreiche*

Theodor von Sosnosky (1866–1930), *Die Politik im Habsburgerreiche. Randglossen zur Zeitgeschichte*. Zweite, durchgesehene Auflage. Berlin: Allgemeiner Verein für Deutsche Literatur (Hermann Paetel Verlag), 1913. A two-volume contemporary survey of roughly seven hundred and sixty pages, drawn from the scanned set preserved in a library copy (Google/Anna's Archive scan, oclc 2179412) via the already-OCR'd Fraktur page texts; the raw text in this wiki is that single complete two-volume scan (762 pages). Sosnosky, a German publicist and political writer in Vienna, wrote deliberately *not* as strict scientific history but as a living "Randglossen" commentary on the politics of the Habsburg monarchy — moved, as his preface says, by the surprising poverty of the German literature on so rich and dramatic a subject, and treating the present and the immediate past.

Dominant years in the text are **1878–1913** (with the long retrospect on Austrian policy from Schwarzenberg onward and the Magyarization politics of the Dualist era).

## Band I — Erster Teil: Äussere Politik

### Vorwort (Band I)

Sosnosky opens on the superabundant, dramatic and passionate material of the politics of the Habsburg monarchy, and on the strange poverty of the German literature devoted to it — pamphlets there are in plenty, but books for the day that treat the subject thoroughly are few, and other languages have more on it than German. He declares that his work "shall not be a history", at least not in the strict scientific sense, but a commentary on the politics of the present and the immediate past.

### I. Von Schwarzenberg bis Aehrenthal

A review of Austria-Hungary's foreign policy from the age of Prince Felix zu Schwarzenberg and the reaction after 1848 down to the chancellorship of Alois Lexa von Aehrenthal (1906–1912) — the retreat from the German Question after 1866, the Eastern orientation, and the shift of the monarchy's weight toward the Balkans that culminated in the Bosnian crisis.

### II. Die Annexion

The annexation of Bosnia and Herzegovina (October 1908) and its international consequences — the Aehrenthal policy that tore up Article 25 of the Treaty of Berlin, the crisis with the Porte, the storm in Serbia and Montenegro, and the stand of the Great Powers.

### III. Österreich und das Deutsche Reich

The relations of the monarchy to the German Empire — the Dual Alliance of 1879 and its working, the standing of Austria-Hungary in Bismarck's and then Wilhelmine Germany's system of alliances, and the growing frictions and dependencies between the two empires.

### IV. Österreich und Italien

Austria-Hungary's relations with Italy — the unredeemed irredenta and the contest for the Adriatic, and the place of the Italian alliance in the Triple Alliance.

## Band I — Zweiter Teil: Innere Politik (in Österreich)

### Einleitung: Das System

The constitutional system of the Austrian half of the monarchy after the Ausgleich — the character of the *Reichsrat* and of the parliamentary life of Cisleithania, the frame within which the national struggles of the Austrian peoples unfolded.

### 1. Der nationale Hader (a) Allgemeines

**Das nationale Prinzip** — the rise of the national principle as the governing force of the age, and its effect upon the multi-national state; **Nationalität und Sprache** — the identification of nation with language and the struggles that followed.

### (b) Besonderes: Die nationalen Strömungen

**I. Alldeutschtum** — the pan-German movement within Austria and its contest against the other nationalities. **II. Panslawismus** — the pan-Slav current, examined in turn among the **Tschechen** (Czechs), **Südslawen** (South Slavs), **Polen**, and **Ruthenen**. **III. Irredenta** — the Italian irredentist movement in the Austrian littoral and Tyrol.

### (c) Resumé

**I. Die Symptome** and **II. Die Ursachen** — the symptoms of the national malady of the Austrian state and its deeper causes, as diagnosed by Sosnosky at the close of the first part.

## Band II — Die Politik in Ungarn

### Vorwort (Band II)

Sosnosky explains the changed disposition of the second band, occasioned by the ever more significant and louder events within the lands of St. Stephen's Crown: he was moved to treat these affairs far more fully than originally intended, and — bound by contractual limits of space — to "amputate" elsewhere in order to keep the treatment of Hungarian conditions from being cramped. He also answers the attacks that certain quarters had made upon his first band: he would not trouble his readers over merely adverse judgments, nor over the personal barbs, but replies to the malicious attempts to injure him in business that accompanied them.

### Einleitung: Magyarische Politik

An opening portrait of Magyar politics and character — the passionate, domineering temper of the Hungarian political nation, its self-absorption, and the attitude toward the non-Magyar peoples of the kingdom.

### Erster Abschnitt: Ungarn und Österreich

**I. Los von Österreich!** — the "Away from Austria!" movement in Hungary, its adherents and its programme. **II. Der Ausgleich** — the Compromise of 1867 as it stood within Hungary, the Deák settlement and its opponents. **III. Ungarn und Österreich** — the real relation of Hungary to Austria under the dualism. **IV. Die Magyaren und die Krone** — the Magyar attitude to the Crown and the monarch. **V. Der Kampf um die Armee** — the long struggle over the common army, its language of command, the Hungarian military demands, and the crisis this provoked.

### Zweiter Abschnitt: Der nationale Hader in Ungarn

**Einleitung: Der "magyarische Nationalstaat"** — the Magyar conception of the national state, against the background of which the subject-nationalities of Hungary were treated.

**I. Die "Nation"** — under **A. Panmagyarismus** and **B. Magyarisierung**: the general theory (the school, the courts, the electoral system, the results of Magyarization, and the "Talmi-Magyaren" — the sham or half Magyarized). **II. Die "Nationalitäten"** — the subject peoples: the **Deutschen** (Germans), the **Rumänen** (Romanians), the **Slowaken** (Slovaks), the **Ruthenen** (Ruthenians), and the **Serben** (Serbs), and the working of the Magyar national state upon each. **III. Kroatien und Ungarn** — Croatia-Slavonia and its relation to the Hungarian kingdom under the nagodba of 1868.

### Resumé: Die österreichische Frage

**I. Die politische Bedeutung des Habsburgerreichs** — the political weight and the raison d'être of the Habsburg monarchy within the European state-system. **II. Die Entwicklungsmöglichkeiten des Habsburgerreichs** — the possible futures of the monarchy as Sosnosky surveyed them on the eve of 1914: (1) **Revision des Dualismus** — a revision of the dualism; (2) **Groß-Ungarn** — the greater-Hungarian solution; (3) **Trialismus** — the trialist reorganization, chiefly with the South Slavs; (4) **Groß-Österreich** — the federal plan (of Aurel Popovici) of a "Greater Austria". His closing argument — reached after weighing these alternatives — is that the future of the monarchy lay in an inner reform that should reconcile the peoples with the state; the work closes on the motto that he held before the Habsburg monarchy: to attain unity through multiplicity, and so to embody the watchword of its ruler — *viribus unitis* ("with united forces").

## Series note

This work stands as the Austrian–Hungarian political thread of the 1866–1919 period: it is a contemporary German account of the internal and external politics of the Dual Monarchy from the age of Bismarck down to the crisis years before Sarajevo, written by a Vienna-based German publicist. It complements the constitutional-juridical Hungarian view of [Beksics, *A dualismus*](beksics-a-dualismus-1892.md) with a German-national perspective on the same monarchy, and it looks forward along the Balkan road that [Sosnosky's own *Die Balkanpolitik Österreich-Ungarns seit 1866*](sosnosky-die-balkanpolitik-osterreich-ungarns-bd1-1914.md) traces in detail, down to the eve of the First World War.
