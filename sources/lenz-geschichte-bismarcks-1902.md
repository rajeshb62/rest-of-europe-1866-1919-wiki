# Max Lenz — *Geschichte Bismarcks*

*Geschichte Bismarcks*, by **Max Lenz** (1850–1932), Professor of History at the University of Berlin, published by Duncker & Humblot, Leipzig, **1902** (OCLC 1158522). The text of this wiki is the HathiTrust full-text scan (University of Wisconsin copy, 472 pages).

In his **Vorwort** (preface, Berlin, 29 June 1902) Lenz describes the work as the almost unchanged separate edition (*Sonderausgabe*) of his article for the *Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie*, with which Freiherr Rochus von Liliencron, the editor of the national work, had entrusted him; the introduction and a few notes on points where he wished to point to more remote sources or had something particular to say were added. He thanks his friends Hans Delbrück and Hermann Oncken, the latter of whom followed the elaboration of the later parts of the book with penetrating criticism.

## Structure

The work proceeds chronologically through Bismarck's life:

1. **Einleitung. Friedrich Wilhelm III. und sein Staat** — the Prussia into which Bismarck was born (his birth in the year of Waterloo).
2. **Jugendjahre** — youth.
3. **Die Revolution** — the Revolution of 1848.
4. **Frankfurt** — the Frankfurt Diet.
5. **Petersburg und Paris. Eintritt ins Ministerium** — Petersburg and Paris, and entry into the ministry (1862).
6. **Die Anfänge des Ministeriums** — the beginning of the ministry.
7. **Polnische Revolution** — the Polish revolution (1863).
8. **Der Kampf mit dem Liberalismus** — the struggle with Liberalism (the constitutional conflict).
9. **Der Frankfurter Fürstentag** — the Frankfurt Congress of Princes (1863).
10. **Schleswig-Holstein** — the Schleswig-Holstein question.
11. **Niederwerfung Österreichs** — the overthrow of Austria (1866).
12. **Norddeutscher Bund** — the North German Confederation.
13. **Hessische und deutsche Frage** — the Hessian and German question (the 1866–1870 years).
14. **Krieg mit Frankreich. Aufrichtung des Deutschen Reiches** — the war with France and the founding of the German Empire (1870–71).
15. **Kulturkampf; Bund und Bruch mit den Liberalen** — the Kulturkampf; the union with and break from the Liberals (the turn of 1879).
16. **Auswärtige Politik im Neuen Reich** — foreign policy in the new Reich (the alliance system).
17. **Innere Politik im letzten Jahrzehnt. Ausgang** — domestic policy in the last decade, and the end: the conflict between Kaiser and Kanzler, the tragic solution of **18 March 1890** (Bismarck's dismissal), his retirement to Friedrichsruh, and his death ("als die Rieseneiche im Sachsenwalde stürzte" — when the giant oak of the Sachsenwald fell, in 1898), closing with a summing-up (*Die Summe*) of his work.

The volume ends with the **Anmerkungen** (notes) section, **Berichtigungen** (corrections), and the printer's colophon.
