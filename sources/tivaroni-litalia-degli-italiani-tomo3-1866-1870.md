# Tivaroni — L'Italia degli Italiani, Tomo III (1866–1870)

**Carlo Tivaroni (1843–1906)** — *Storia critica del risorgimento italiano. L'Italia degli Italiani*. Tomo III (1866–1870). — Torino: Roux Frassati e Co., 1897.

The third and final volume of the series-within-the-work bearing the title *L'Italia degli Italiani*, and the closing volume of the whole *Storia critica del risorgimento italiano*. It opens with the war of 1866 against Austria, which completed the acquisition of the Veneto, and carries the story through the long struggle of the Roman question to the breach of Porta Pia and the annexation of Rome (20 September 1870) — the last great act of Italian unification. The volume then turns from narrative to retrospect.

Tivaroni divides the volume into four *Parti* (Parte XI through Parte XIV, continuing the numbering from the two previous volumes of the series). The first two treat the war of 1866 and the Roman question; the third is a synthetic account of the development of the national idea across the whole Risorgimento; the fourth and last offers a gallery of biographical portraits of the leading figures of the movement, closing with the three great protagonists — Cavour, Garibaldi and Victor Emmanuel II.

The volume is approximately 550 pages of text, extracted from a University of California scan (Anna's Archive). It is the 572-page digitization.

## Content Summary

### Parte XI — La guerra del 1866

The third war of Italian independence, fought in alliance with Prussia.

- The treaty of alliance with **Prussia**
- The battle of **Custoza** (24 June 1866)
- The inaction after Custoza
- **Garibaldi** in the **Trentino**
- The naval battle of **Lissa** (20 July 1866)
- The *cadeau du Veneto* and the rising of Palermo

### Parte XII — La questione romana

The long struggle over Rome between the Italian Kingdom and the Holy See.

- The **Syllabus** (8 December 1864)
- The ministry of **Urbano Rattazzi** (11 April – 19 October 1867)
- The conspiracy in Rome
- **Mentana** (3 November 1867)
- The ministry of **Luigi Federico Menabrea** (27 October 1867 – 19 November 1869)
- The ministry of **Giovanni Lanza** and **Quintino Sella** (15 December 1869 – 20 September 1870)
- The negotiations for alliance with Austria and France
- The **20 September 1870** — the breach of Porta Pia and the occupation of Rome
- Conclusion

### Parte XIII — Lo svolgimento del pensiero nazionale

A retrospective survey of the growth of the national idea across the Risorgimento.

- The height of **Mazzini**'s prestige
- The germs of monarchical unity (**Vincenzo Gioberti**)
- The *murattismo*
- The *Società Nazionale Italiana*
- The year 1859
- The decisive year (1860)
- The moderates and the *partito d'azione*
- Culture in the Risorgimento
- The question of Rome
- The good and the evil

### Parte XIV — Gli uomini del Risorgimento

A gallery of biographical portraits of the principal figures of the movement.

- **Nino Bixio**, **Agostino Bertani**, **Francesco Crispi**, **Benedetto Cairoli**, **Giorgio Pallavicino**, **Cesare Correnti**, **Antonio Scialoja**, **Silvio Spaventa**, **Giuseppe Pasolini**, **Aurelio Saffi**, **Agostino Depretis**, **Carlo Matteucci**, **Francesco Arese**, **Mariano D'Ayala**
- The **Cardinal Antonelli**
- **Massimo D'Azeglio**, **Angelo Brofferio**, **Giovanni Lanza**, **Quintino Sella**, **Marco Minghetti**, **Urbano Rattazzi**, **Alfonso Lamarmora**, **Luigi Carlo Farini**
- The **Barone Ricasoli** — the first part of his life; Ricasoli as *unitario*
- The **Conte di Cavour** — his youth; the journalist, deputy and minister; the judgements upon him; his mind; the judgement of posterity
- The **Generale Garibaldi** — the adventurer; the hero; Garibaldi the general; his doctrines; Garibaldi at Caprera; the judgement of history
- **Vittorio Emanuele** — *Re Galantuomo*; the good and intelligent King; the first King of Italy

**Note on the text:** The OCR is Italian. The printed *Indice* appears near the close of the volume. Tivaroni writes throughout as a historian of the national movement, and this closing volume is the most explicitly retrospective of the series: the final Parti look back across the whole Risorgimento and sum up the significance of its principal actors, treating the completion of unification in 1870 as the culmination of the national achievement.
