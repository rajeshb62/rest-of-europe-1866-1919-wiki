# Conrad von Hötzendorf — *Aus meiner Dienstzeit 1906–1918*, Band V: Oktober, November, Dezember 1914

Franz Graf Conrad von Hötzendorf (1852–1925), *Aus meiner Dienstzeit 1906–1918*. Fünfter Band: *Oktober, November, Dezember 1914*. Wien, Berlin, Leipzig, München: Rikola Verlag, 1925. This final volume carries the account from 1 October 1914 to the close of the first war year. The raw text in this wiki is the scanned copy (University of Michigan library; Rikola Verlag 1925), 1022 pages, already OCR'd, concatenated in page order.

## Vorwort

Conrad explains that, ill for more than a year, he had been unable to complete the manuscript of Band V as he wished in the autumn of the previous year, and the volume therefore appears late. Its predecessor, Band IV, ends with the preparation of the Austro-Hungarian armies and the German eastern army for the renewed offensive against Russia and with the first events of the recommenced offensive against Serbia, closing with 30 September 1914. Band V continues the account of events in direct succession, beginning therefore with 1 October, and reaches to the close of the first war year 1914. Like the others, this volume claims to be only a single, rough-hewn building stone, a small part of the great material out of which future historians will have to erect the structure that may be called an objective representation of the world war. He hopes so. But should they set the one-sided, chauvinist-propagandist purpose of historiography, under the cloak of patriotic duty, higher than scientific objectivity, nobility and dignity, and should they deliberately turn the torch of truth to the ground and stifle it, then the hope of a great objective work will remain in vain and posterity will be richer in many a delusion. He has kept, in Band V as in Band IV and for the same reasons, to the day-by-day rendering of events, which alone can give the reader a halfway accurate picture of the progress of the military action. The supreme leader in war must be ready to act at every hour of day and night and be prepared to see everything he has planned and ordered frustrated. His memoir work is meant to leave the historian an ordered, intellectually connected document material, and also to offer the professional soldier the possibility of coming to know the war and its conduct at the highest point in its innermost connections and often decisive details.

## The renewed offensive in Galicia and Poland; battles at the San and on the Vistula

The volume's principal subject is the renewed offensive in Galicia and Poland; the battles at the San and on the Vistula; the retreat of the allies in Poland and Galicia; the breaking forth of the Russian masses over the Vistula; the battles at Łódź–Lowicz, Krakau and Limanowa–Łapanów; the advance of the allies; the fighting in Poland; the battles at the San and near Sambor; the fighting in the Carpathians; the renewed advance of the Russians; the limitation of both sides' advance; and the temporary position warfare from the Baltic to the frontier of Rumania.

## The second offensive and the setback in Serbia

The volume also covers the second offensive and the setback in Serbia, month by month for October, November and December, closing with a retrospect on the battles of Łódź, Krakau and Limanowa–Łapanów.

## Charakteristik der Lage am Jahresschluß

Conrad summarizes the situation at the close of the year. Against Russia: Group Pflanzer in stubborn, energetic small war on the forest-Carpathian wall, holding the important Uzsok Pass, with Przemysl in active defence binding strong forces of the besieger; the 3rd Army's eastern group not only checking the enemy's advance but itself in successful attack, while the main body of the army gave way before the strong pressure of the superior enemy; the 4th and 1st Armies stubbornly holding their fronts; the German 9th Army thrown into the defensive and expecting reinforcements. On the Balkan front, the Balkan forces drawn together in the 5th Army were, after the failed offensive, recovering behind the Save and Danube; Bosnia was secured by special groups, and the small war against Montenegro was successfully in progress. On New Year's Eve he drove with Lieutenant-Colonel Kundmann to Berlin, departing by special train from Teschen at 10 in the evening, toward the new year.

## Rückblick auf das erste Kriegsjahr und dessen Vorgeschichte

Under the headings of war guilt (Kriegsschuld) and the causes of the war (Kriegsursachen), Conrad insists that this retrospect can only touch the most essential in a fleeting reference. To grasp the thousandfold connections that in their final result led to the world war, the laborious path remains of following, in strict objectivity and on the basis of far-reaching historical knowledge, the rich and ever-growing material that the literature has offered. A complete connection will only be researchable until the archives of the enemies also open. Whoever goes this way unprejudiced, guided only by the drive for truth, will soon come to the insight that there can be no question of war guilt, and that the world war, too, is to be valued as one of those unavoidable transformations that spring from the developmental drive of states and peoples and from the resulting struggle for existence. Once one reaches this height of conception, every seeking of a guilt for the war appears an idle, even petty undertaking. Only the fathoming of the causes of the war can form the field of historical research; it must proceed from the struggle for existence as the world-dominating principle. This is not, he adds, an excuse for the means employed by statesmen, politicians and regents to reach their goals: the whole Great-Serbian policy, bloody and murder-stained in its procedure, must always be regarded with disgust and contempt. A politics that aims only at preserving peace must in the end lead to ruin and defeat; only a politics with positive goals, resolved on struggle when chances offer and skilled in creating, bringing about and exploiting those chances, makes it possible to survive the struggle for existence victoriously. Aging monarchs and statesmen resting on inherited laurels commonly lack the inner force for this; but nature sees to it that young opponents arise who reach actively over them into the wheel of history. So, he writes, was the policy of our opponents. Setting against the conservative, merely preservative foreign policy of Kaiser Franz Joseph and the no less peace-loving Kaiser Wilhelm II the busy policy of a Poincaré, the aggressive policy of a Pašić, the sly policy of a Bratianu, the faithless far-reaching policy of a Salandra, San Giuliano and Sonnino, the coldly calculating policy of England, and finally the deed-hungry policy of the Russian Panslavists who went their own ways over the head of the weak Tsar, one may find the connection that led to Germany's and Austria-Hungary's isolation and defeat. The shots fired on 28 June 1914 against the heir to the throne of Austria-Hungary tore the trustful out of this deception; it was too late. After the Berlin Congress (1878), Bismarck thought to drive Europe from the box, but already forces were stirring around him that pressed toward a transformation of the world situation. Even at the end of the nineteenth century a transformation of the grouping of the powers in Europe had begun that could be called the encirclement of Germany and Austria-Hungary, whose aim was to paralyse and shatter these two powers.

## Schlußwort

Conrad closes by defending the k. u. k. army against the belittlements that had come to it from the German side during the war, stating that as the oldest general of the old army, in which he served forty-seven years in war and peace, he felt the duty to correct legends of which it was to be feared they would be stamped as facts and taken over as such into history. He wished in particular to protect the k. u. k. army against those lightly thrown, spiteful phrases hurled at it by men who wandered in the labyrinth of arrogance. He was not blind to the defects of the old army, as numerous passages of his work show; but in his defence he was also guided by the wish not to let irritations arise between the Germans and Austrians, which would be the more misplaced as, surrounded on all sides by enemies, they were more than ever dependent on keeping their concord.

## Anlagenverzeichnis

The appendix (Anlagen and maps A–E and 1–35) lies in a separate pocket given with this volume. It includes plans of the fortresses of Przemysl and Krakau, the order of battle and strength of the k. u. k. armies on the Russian theatre of war (early October, mid-November, early December 1914), and a series of situation documents for the own and the presumed enemy positions on the Russian and Serbian theatres of war through October, November and December 1914.
