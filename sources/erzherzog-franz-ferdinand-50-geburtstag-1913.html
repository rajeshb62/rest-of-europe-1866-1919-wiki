Erzherzog Franz Ferdinand, unser Thronfolger — Illustriertes Sonderheft der Österreichischen Rundschau, zum 50. Geburtstag

Erzherzog Franz Ferdinand, unser Thronfolger. Zum 50. Geburtstag. Illustriertes Sonderheft der Österreichischen Rundschau. Wien und Leipzig: K.u.K. Hof-Buchdruckerei und Verlags-Buchhandlung Carl Fromme; für Deutschland Georg Stilke, Hofbuchhändler Sr. K.u.K. Hoheit des Kronprinzen, Berlin. Issued 18 December 1913 (the archduke’s fiftieth birthday). Editors (Herausgeber): Leopold Freiherr von Chlumecky, Dr. Karl Glossy, Dr. Felix Freiherr von Oppenheimer; verantwortlicher Redakteur Karl Junker. Copyright 18 December 1913 by Österreichische Rundschau, Wien. A series of eight essays and illustrated plates on the heir to the Habsburg throne, drawn from the scanned copy (University of Toronto; Google scan) via tesseract Fraktur OCR (German) of the 120 scanned pages; the raw text in this wiki is that OCR.

The special issue opens by recalling that in a few days Austria-Hungary’s future emperor and king would complete his fiftieth year, and that more than ever all eyes turned upon the man who would one day be called to direct the destinies of the realm. Its several contributions, by the general, the admirals, the university professors and the chamberlain who knew him, together present the heir of the house of Habsburg as soldier, sailor, patron of art and science, hunter and collector.

Unser Thronfolger (Leopold Freiherr von Chlumecky)

Chlumecky reflects on the peculiar burden of the Habsburg ruler: in this many-shaped realm the crown is a heavier burden than anywhere else, for here the dynasty itself is the foundation of a structure that is not always logically and organically articulated — elsewhere the state carries the dynasty, here the dynasty carries the state. Austria-Hungary’s sovereign cannot confine himself within the narrow limits sometimes drawn by doctrinaire constitutional conceptions. Chlumecky sets out the theme that runs through the whole special issue: the future emperor as the bond that encircles and unites the divergent elements of the monarchy.

Erzherzog Franz Ferdinand (Theodor von Sosnosky)

Sosnosky gives the biographical portrait. Franz Ferdinand Karl Ludwig Joseph Maria was born on 18 December 1863 in the Herberstein palace in Graz, a Christmas gift to his parents, Archduke Karl Ludwig (brother of Kaiser Franz Joseph, son of Archduke Franz Karl and Archduchess Sophie, born Princess of Bavaria) and Archduchess Maria Annunziata (daughter of King Ferdinand II of the Two Sicilies, of the house of Bourbon). By this descent, as a genealogist had shown, the archduke united in his veins the blood of no fewer than one hundred and twelve ancestral houses (seventy-one German, twenty Polish, eight French, seven Italian and six of various other nations), which in eleven generations counted 2047 verifiable ancestors — making the heir one of the most richly-ancestored princes in Europe. At the time of his birth his father lived as a private man in Graz, having withdrawn two years earlier from political life. Sosnosky traces the upbringing and formation of the future heir. (The world journey, which he undertook in December 1892, was a decisive turning-point in his life.)

Der Thronfolger als Soldat (General der Infanterie Emil Voinovich Edler von Belobreska)

Voinovich, a general of infantry, presents the heir as soldier, and grounds his martial zeal in the traditions of the dynasty — the founder Kaiser Rudolf, Maximilian the knight, Charles V, the devout Ferdinand II, and Maria Theresia, who though but a weak woman bore the heart of a king. On such a foundation, he argues, a lively feeling for the greatness of the dynasty and the realm fills the archduke; his wide travels taught him to know foreign lands and peoples, yet he kept his love of the fatherland — glowing even more after his great journey he loves the great, beautiful realm he is called one day to rule. Convinced that the foremost means to the greatness of the state lies in the sharpening of the sword, he gave the chief weight to the armed power of the monarchy, labouring without rest to raise it.

Der Thronfolger und die Marine (Konteradmiral Franz Mittl)

Rear-Admiral Mittl recalls that the ruling house of the Habsburgs counts many names bound up with the development of seafaring — Charles V, in whose realm the sun never set, and whose rule only a sovereign could boast in whose realm the navy held a dominating position; and later Kaiser Karl VI, Maria Theresia and Kaiser Franz Joseph I, whose names remain forever linked to the flourishing of the seafaring of the Danubian monarchy. But the manifold tasks of the head of state did not permit these bearers of the crown to enter into all the details of that peculiar machinery, so remote from ordinary life; it was reserved for the heir to the throne to give the navy his more particular care. Mittl sets out the archduke’s role in fostering the fleet.

Erzherzog Franz Ferdinand als Kunstfreund (Professor Dr. Georg Graf Mycielski)

Mycielski, professor at the university of Cracow, presents the heir as a friend of art, and dwells on the collections he built up at his castle of Konopišt in Bohemia. Three special collections are singled out. The greatest creation is the armoury in the upper storey of the castle, where the archduke placed the most precious and rarest treasures of the whole Este collection (which he was continually enriching by new purchases) — armour, cannon and old Este standards; the early and late Renaissance triumphs there in a series of complete suits of armour with the finest gold and silver inlay and extraordinarily fine reliefs and engravings of Italian and Augsburg workmanship, among them the charming work of Benvenuto Cellini, rare shields and helmets, and complete horse-armour for tournament or combat — an armoury (Armeria Areiducale in Konopišt) that is a pearl among the European collections of its kind, in its chief stock an inheritance of the famous ancestors from Ferrara, enriched and completed by the archduke with new objects, chiefly from the Renaissance period. A small but exceedingly interesting special collection fills a square chamber of the tower exclusively with powder-horns and small horns of the most varied kinds from all epochs, finely wrought. A third collection is devoted to the archduke’s hunting trophies from his travels.

Der Thronfolger und die Wissenschaft (Hofrat Professor R. von Wettstein)

Wettstein (Richard von Wettstein, botanist) treats the heir’s relation to science. The archduke had taken the protectorate of the academies of sciences in Prague and Cracow, so that the prince nearest the throne stood in intimate relation to the three highest central institutions of the realm devoted only to science. He brought a quite special interest to a series of sciences — in the first place the natural sciences, especially botany, and likewise folklore and the study of home (Volks- und Heimatkunde). His love of nature went back to the impressions of his youth in the Lower-Austrian Voralpen. After journeys through much of Europe and then to Egypt, Syria and Palestine, he undertook his great world journey in 1892, returning with rich ethnographic and natural-scientific collections, which under his continued personal participation were first housed in the upper Belvedere, then in the Modena-Palais in Vienna, and finally in the new building of the Hofburg. At the botanical exhibition of 1905 at Schönbrunn (with the international botanical congress, whose protectorate he held) and at the opening in 1913 of the Austrian Adriatic exhibition (also under his protectorate) he surprised the specialists with his detailed knowledge. His interest in natural science was intimately bound up with a love of horticulture, whose rise in Austria in these years is directly or indirectly owing to his fostering and purposeful intervention; at Konopišt he created over twenty years, under difficult conditions, a park of some 340 hectares, renowned both for the grandeur of the layout and for its artistic and loving treatment of detail.

Die Weltreise des Erzherzogs Franz Ferdinand (Konteradmiral Alfred Freiherr von Koudelka)

Rear-Admiral Koudelka recounts the world journey. On 15 December 1892 the archduke sailed from Europe to travel round the world. The journey, which he undertook in his twenty-ninth year, was a decisive turning-point in his life — it was that great journey that gave his later years their direction and set before him his task; after it he became a frequent visitor of Brioni and of the coast. Koudelka, who accompanied the heir on his voyage, describes the route and the impressions gathered on it, and the abiding effect the journey had upon the future emperor.

Erzherzog Franz Ferdinand als Jäger (Camillo Morgan)

Morgan, the master of the hunt, presents the heir as hunter. In green Styria, where the archduke’s cradle stood, the old noble chase (Edelgejaid) had ever flourished — there Duke Frederick the Belligerent had his hunting-seat Dobl, the hunting-horns of Duke Ernst the Iron and of his consort Cimburgis of Masovia, won at a bear-hunt, had rung out, and Kaiser Maximilian I, the “last knight” and “great-mighty woodman”, had thrown his hunting-knife after the swift chamois. Morgan suggests these three hunt-loving rulers may have stood by the cradle of Archduke Franz Ferdinand and begged for him the gift of St. Hubertus, that he become as true a hunter as they — and he did. His aged imperial uncle on the old Habsburg throne himself called him “much-tried huntsman”. His trophies grew to the colossal; by the thousands mounted the red-deer antlers, the falls of the fallow-deer, the chamois beards, the roe antlers, the boar’s jaws and the woodcock’s feathers and the pelts of beasts of prey; to these trophies of home game were added the elephants and tiger trophies from India (from his world journey), the bear trophies from the preserves of the Kaiser of Russia, the bison (Wisent) and so on. Morgan closes with the dedication of Austria’s huntsmen to the heir, and quotes the “Thronfolger-Hymne” set to music by the composer Tresdorn, with its invocation of the holy hunt-patron St. Hubertus, prayed by the hunters of the realm for the future bearer of the crown — “so far as the double eagle’s wings beat”.
