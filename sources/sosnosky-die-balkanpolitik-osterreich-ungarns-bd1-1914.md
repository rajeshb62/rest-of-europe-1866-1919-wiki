# Sosnosky — *Die Balkanpolitik Österreich-Ungarns seit 1866*, Band I

Theodor von Sosnosky (1866–1930), *Die Balkanpolitik Österreich-Ungarns seit 1866*. Erster Band. Stuttgart: Deutsche Verlags-Anstalt, 1915. With two maps and an appendix. A contemporary account of some three hundred and thirty pages, drawn from the scanned copy (University of California, Berkeley; Google/Anna's Archive scan, oclc 2757943) via the already-OCR'd Fraktur page texts; the raw text in this wiki is that scan (330 pages). As Sosnosky states in his preface, the purpose of the work is a clear, connected account of the share that the Habsburg monarchy took in the Oriental Question from 1866 down to the most recent Balkan crisis; because the causal connection of the events would not appear unless the historical background were supplied, he opened with a retrospect (Rückblicke), and he dwelled with deliberate emphasis on the military side — recalling that war is nothing but the continuation of politics by other means.

Dominant years in the text are **1866–1878** (with the long retrospect to 1526), the decisive term being the occupation of Bosnia in 1878.

## Vorwort

Sosnosky sets out the aim of the whole work — a connected narrative of the Habsburg share in the Oriental Question from 1866 to the most recent Balkan crisis — and justifies the historical retrospect with which Band I opens, without which the account would hang in the air. He explains why some episodes (the Crimean crisis, the diplomatic mine-war that preceded the occupation of Bosnia) are treated only briefly, and why the military side is so strongly emphasized: war is the continuation of politics by other means. He cites the example of Friedjung's *Der Kampf um die Vorherrschaft in Deutschland*, whose second volume is nearly all military history, and remarks that a statesman such as Kaiser Wilhelm I rose to his historical greatness only with Roon and Moltke beside him to translate his great thoughts into great deeds.

## Erster Abschnitt: Rückblicke

**I. Die Angriffskriege der Türken, 1526–1718** — the age of Turkish attack: from Mohács (1526) through the sieges of Vienna and the long wars of reconquest, down to the treaties that rolled back the Ottoman advance. **II. Die Verteidigungskriege der Türken, 1719–1792** — the period of Turkish defence, as the monarchy and Russia pressed the retreat of the Porte in the eighteenth century. **III. Von Kaunitz bis Beust, 1792–1866** — Austria's Balkan policy from Kaunitz to Beust: (1) **Der serbische Aufstand** — the Serbian rising under Karađorđe and Miloš; (2) **Die griechische Frage** — the Greek war of independence; (3) **Konservierungspolitik** — Austria's conserving, status-quo policy in the East; (4) **Der neue Kurs** — the new course; (5) **Österreich im Krimkriege** — Austria's part in the Crimean War.

## Zweiter Abschnitt: Der Aufstand der Bocchesen

The insurrection of the Bokelj (Bocchesi) in the Bay of Kotor in 1869 — an episode to which Sosnosky devotes a special section because of its dramatic and exemplary character in the history of the monarchy's South-Slav policy.

## Dritter Abschnitt: Die bosnische Frage

**I. Nach 1866** — Bosnia-Herzegovina in the Austrian horizon after Königgrätz. **II. Vor dem Sturm** — the gathering of the crisis. **III. Die Zustände in Bosnien und in der Herzegovina** — the condition of Bosnia and Herzegovina under Turkish rule: the anarchy, the agrarian misery, the confessional hatreds. **IV. Sturmboten** — the portents of the storm. **V. Österreich und Bosnien** — the Austrian claims and policy toward Bosnia. **VI. Andrássys Balkanprogramm** — the Balkan programme of Gyula Andrássy. **VII. Beschwichtigungsversuche** — the attempts at appeasement. **VIII. Von Reichstadt nach Konstantinopel** — from the Reichstadt agreement with Russia to the diplomacy at Constantinople. **IX. Österreich-Ungarn und Rußland** — the Austro-Russian rivalry and understanding. **X. Andrássys Balkanpolitik und die Volksvertretungen** — Andrássy's Balkan policy before the Hungarian and Austrian parliaments. **XI. Der Berliner Kongreß** — the Congress of Berlin (1878) and the mandate given to Austria-Hungary to occupy and administer Bosnia-Herzegovina. **XII. Vor dem Einmarsch** — the eve of the entry of the occupation troops.

## Vierter Abschnitt: Die Okkupation

The military occupation of Bosnia and Herzegovina in the late summer of 1878: **I. Der Schauplatz der Okkupation** — the theatre of operations; **II. Die Streitkräfte der Insurgenten** — the forces of the insurgents who met the Austro-Hungarian army; **III. Die militärischen Vorbereitungen Österreich-Ungarns** — the military preparations of the monarchy; **IV. Die Mittelkolonne** — the central column; **V. Die rechte Kolonne** — the right column; **VI. Die linke Kolonne** — the left column; **VII. In der Herzegovina** — the operations in Herzegovina; **VIII. Der Marsch auf Sarajevo** — the march on Sarajevo; **IX. Die Einnahme von Sarajevo** — the capture of Sarajevo (19 August 1878); **X. Neugruppierung der Streitkräfte** — the regrouping of the forces; **XI. In der Posavina und Župa** — the operations in the Posavina and the Župa; **XII. Wieder in der Herzegovina** — the renewed fighting in Herzegovina; **XIII. In Südbosnien** — in southern Bosnia; **XIV. In Nordwestbosnien** — in northwestern Bosnia; **XV. Heimkehr** — the return home; **XVI. Resumé** — the summing-up of the campaign, its cost and its meaning.

## Anhang (Appendix)

**I. Denkschrift des Feldmarschalls Grafen Radetzky vom 30. August 1856** — the memorandum of Field Marshal Count Radetzky of 30 August 1856, on the military and strategic value of Bosnia and the South-Slav lands. **II. Ordre de bataille der Okkupationstruppen, Anfang August 1878** — the order of battle of the occupation troops at the beginning of August 1878. Followed by a Personenregister (index of persons).

## Series note

This is the first band of Sosnosky's two-volume account of the Habsburg monarchy's Balkan policy. It carries the Eastern thread of the 1866–1919 period from the aftermath of Königgrätz through the great crisis of 1878 — the Congress of Berlin and the occupation of Bosnia — which is the very hinge of the period's Eastern Question, and it pairs with the second band, [*Die Balkanpolitik Österreich-Ungarns seit 1866*, Band II](sosnosky-die-balkanpolitik-osterreich-ungarns-bd2-1914.md), which carries the same policy from the Berlin settlement down to the Peace of Bukarest (1913). It is the Austrian counterpart, along the Eastern road, of [Sosnosky's own *Die Politik im Habsburgerreiche*](sosnosky-die-politik-im-habsburgerreiche-1913.md) and of [Hanotaux's account of the Eastern Question through Berlin](hanotaux-histoire-france-contemporaine-t4.md).
