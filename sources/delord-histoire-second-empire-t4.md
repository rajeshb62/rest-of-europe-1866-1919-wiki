# Delord — Histoire illustrée du second empire, Tome IV

**Taxile Delord (1815–1877)** — *Histoire illustrée du second empire.* — **Tome quatrième.** — Nouvelle édition. — Paris: Librairie Germer Baillière et Cie, 108, boulevard Saint-Germain, **1880** (printer: Coulommiers, Typographie Paul Brodard). 59 gravures dans le texte.

Republican journalist and deputy (*Siècle*), Delord writes a contemporary opposition history of the Second Empire as a continuous narrative, not memoir. This is the **illustrated edition** (Baillière [1880–83]) of the same multi-tome work originally published 1869–75. The present volume opens with the **1864 legislative session** (Thiers and Rouher debating the "liberties necessary", Ollivier separates from the opposition) and carries the narrative through the Mexican expedition (1864–1865), the Algerian voyage, the climactic **1866** war between Prussia and Austria (**Sadowa, Custozza, Treaty of Prague**), the close of the Mexican expedition, and a long chapter on the clergy in France (1863–1867: the Syllabus, Encyclical, Protestant internal struggles).

**Core chronological span** is **1864–1867** — the liberal Empire's middle years, the Austro-Prussian War as the European watershed, the winding-down of the Mexican adventure, and the religious conflicts (Ultramontane Syllabus versus liberal Catholicism, the Protestant orthodox-liberal schism).

## Chapters

*Opening of the 1864 session; Emperor's discourse. Senate address: petition on publicity of municipal council sessions, petition on irreligious writings, Renan before the Senate. Corps législatif verification of powers: election of Casimir Périer (Thuillier discourse), Lavertujon. Jurisprudence of the majority. The 300-million loan; Berryer's discourse. Address debate: amendments of the Left and Centre-Left. Thiers's discourse; Rouher's reply — the "liberties necessary". Jules Favre's discourse; amendment against official candidacies; Thiers ripostes to Rouher. Abrogation of the coalition law. Pelletan and the municipal regime of Paris and Lyon. Trial of the Thirteen. Foreign policy debate: Schleswig-Holstein and Mexico. Election of Bravay. Alliance of Morny and Ollivier. Ollivier separates from the opposition. Jules Simon's amendment. Jules Favre and Émile Ollivier. Thiers on the budget. Berryer's discourse; Rouher's reply. Ernest Picard and Pelletan.*

### Chapitre II — L'Expédition du Mexique (1864–1865)

*Ball given by General Bazaine in honour of the Emperor and Empress. Coldness of relations between Maximilian and the French. Maximilian at odds with the clergy; he is not sure of the army. Maximilian seeks financial resources; French claims; the Jecker bonds. Maximilian's programme; formation of the Bureau de l'Esprit public. Maximilian requests a police personnel from Paris. Military situation: French occupation confined to a few points. Colonel Dupin's exploits. Maximilian undertakes an interior journey. Hostility of the Holy See; arrival of the nuncio at Vera-Cruz. Roman court's demands. Financial mission of two Mexican bankers to Paris; de Germiny. The Mexican loan patronised by Rouher. Napoleon III's letter to Bazaine. Bazaine exercises a real dictatorship. Maximilian adopts the grandson of Iturbide. Cruel position of his government. Bazaine's marriage. Juarez crosses the border. Maximilian's letter to Napoleon III. Seward's note on the Mexican Empire and French occupation.*

### Chapitre III — L'Année 1865

*State of opinion at the beginning of 1865. Receptions at the Tuileries. Death of Proudhon. Prohibition of the Barthélemy hall conferences. Duruy's report on primary instruction; he is disavowed. Death of Morny. Publication of Napoleon III's *Histoire de César* (volume I). Death of Cobden. Death of Lincoln. Voyage of the Emperor to Algeria; Prince Napoléon's discourse at Ajaccio; emotion caused in official circles. The Montmorency trial; the Sandon affair. Prince Napoléon resigns all his functions. The coachmen's strike. Municipal elections. Death of Palmerston. The Berne congress; the Liège congress. Death of Leopold, king of the Belgians. Death of Lamoricière. Death of Dupin. The committee of Nancy. Imperial letter to Prince Napoléon.*

### Chapitre IV — L'Algérie (1830–1868)

*Projected imperial voyage to Algeria. Retrospective glance at the history of Algeria. State of the colony under the 1830 monarchy, under the 1848 Republic, under the presidency of Louis-Napoléon. Algeria after the coup d'état. General Randon named governor-general. Expedition against the Kabylie of Djurjura. Prince Napoléon minister of Algeria. Maréchal Pélissier governor-general. Imperial letter of 6 February 1863. Senatus-consult of 2 April of the same year. Insurrection of the Saharan tribes. Civil power and military power. Decree of 7 July 1864. The Emperor sails for Algiers; he visits the provinces. Return to France; results of the voyage.*

### Chapitre V — Session de 1865

*Opening of the legislative session. Imperial discourse. Senate: address discussion. Troplong and de Boissy. Relations between Church and State; discourses of Rouland, Mgr de Bonnechose, and Bonjean. Petition discussion: transfer of Charles X's ashes; the hereditary Senate; prostitution; homeopathy; Dupin's discourse. Close of the Senate session. Corps législatif: formation of the bureau; verification of powers. Address debate: Ollivier takes another step toward the government. Latour-Dumoulin. 28 March séance: Thiers's discourse; Thuillier's diatribe in reply. Election in the 3rd circonscription of the Gard. Article-by-article discussion of the address. Opposition amendment; Jules Favre forced to renounce the floor; Ollivier secures adjournment. The Left decides to refrain from further debate; evening meeting of the Left reverses the decision. Law on press offences; electoral liberty. Electoral questions; financial questions. Amendment on the right of testament; election of mayors. Ernest Picard and the city of Paris. Mexico; Algeria; the Italian question. Closure of the address debate. Reading of the address to the Emperor; his reply to the deputation. Provisional liberty. Secondary instruction. The contingent. The budget.*

### Chapitre VI — La Politique Extérieure pendant l'Année 1865

*Retrospective glance at foreign affairs. Difficult situation of Prussia. Austria's embarrassments. Note of the Moniteur du soir. Salzburg interview. Rupture of the European equilibrium. Convention of Gastein. Prussia's anxieties. Bismarck's voyage to Biarritz. Napoleon III's dream — at what price will he sell his neutrality between Prussia and Austria? 1 January 1866 reception. Revolution in Spain. Restoration of the tribune in the Corps législatif. Election in the Bas-Rhin; Laboulaye candidacy. The Auxerre discourse. Napoleon III's double policy. Death of Flocon. *Opening of the session; Emperor's discourse. Senate address: Boissy and President Troplong. Mexico; the 15 September Convention. Rouher's declaration. Persigny's discourse. ### Chapitre VIII — L'Année 1866

*Prussia and Austria in the first months of 1866. Prussia's threatening dispatch to Austria. War council at Berlin. Napoleon III's attitude. Discouragement of the friends of peace. The small German states incline indirectly toward Austria. Bismarck tries to make Napoleon III declare his intentions. Difficulties raised by the king of Prussia. Bismarck fears French intervention. Vienna cabinet proposals to Berlin. Prussian army put on war footing. Prussian army enters Saxony, Hanover, and Electoral Hesse. Wilhelm I sides with his minister. War council of 7 March at Vienna. Austrian military preparations. The Bamberg conferences. Austria wrongly believes she can count on the small states. Bavaria's decision. Military convention between Austria and the Confederate states. Bismarck launches his plan of federal reform. State of the Prussian army. The Austrian army and its heterogeneous composition. Hanover and Hesse in Prussian hands. Benedek's errors. Position of Austrian forces. Combat of Munchengraetz. Crown Prince of Prussia enters Bohemia. Engagements through daylight into the night of 27 June. Clam-Gallaz abandons the Iser line. The Crown Prince debouches onto the Nachod plateau. Moral effect of Nachod. Wilhelm I and Bismarck join the army.*

### Chapitre IX — Sadowa

*Benedek wants to retake the Iser line. Clam-Gallaz, with the 1st Corps and the Saxons, establishes himself at Gitschin. Attack on Gitschin; Clam-Gallaz ordered to rejoin the main army. Silesian army repulsed. Critical situation of the Prussians. Benedek fights partial combats instead of a general action. He falls back on Königgrätz. The armies of the Crown Prince and Prince Frederick-Charles effect their junction. Benedek withdraws his army. Benedetti joins the king of Prussia at army headquarters. Prussian HQ orders the attack. The king's anxiety. Appearance of the Royal Guard. Capture of Chlum by the Guard. Benedek's general offensive fails. Prince Frederick-Charles advances and pushes the Austrian army back toward Sadowa. Benedek's disorderly retreat on Königgrätz. The Prussian army is forced to halt for a day. Sadowa, one of the bloodiest battles of the century.*

### Chapitre X — Custozza

*Relations of Prussia and Piedmont. Rupture between Prussia and Austria. Italy joins Prussia in asking Napoleon III to remain neutral. Gastein Convention. The Venetian question. Bismarck and Italy. Francis Joseph and the grand council at Berlin. General Govone's mission to Berlin; Prussian proposed treaty. Count Arèse's mission to Paris. Napoleon III's discontent. Useless proposition of a congress. Opening of hostilities. Formation of the two armies. Archduke Albert concentrates his army on the Adige. Victor-Emmanuel enters the Quadrilateral. Sudden Austrian attack. General La Marmora directs his reinforcements on Custozza, the centre of the battle. Italian defeat; retreat of the Italian army.*

### Chapitre XI — Le Traité de Prague

*Benedek withdraws on Olmütz. Francis Joseph accepts Napoleon III's mediation. Prussia presses its march on Vienna. Benedetti at Prussian army headquarters. The Prussians within two leagues of Vienna. Five-day truce. Prussia and the federal army. General Vogel von Falkenstein's mission. Combat of Kissingen. Capture of Frankfurt. General Manteuffel concludes an armistice with the secondary states. End of the war. Bismarck preoccupies himself with possible French compensations. Austria makes double integrity of its territory and Saxony's a sine qua non of peace. Nikolsburg preliminaries. Signature of peace at Prague. Political results of the war.*

### Chapitre XII — Les Suites de Sadowa

*Moniteur note announcing mediation. Enthusiasm of brokers and journalists. The Empire not ready for war. First overture regarding Belgium. Napoleon III's double game. Drouyn de Lhuys replaced by Moustier. Draft treaty of which Benedetti imprudently leaves the outline in Bismarck's hands. Bismarck turns toward Russia. Optimistic circular of La Valette. Napoleon III at Vichy. The Luxembourg question. Arrival of the Empress of Mexico at Paris. Ratification of the treaty between Austria and France for the cession of Venetia. The Geneva congress. The new military organisation. Fould's report on the financial situation. Manifesto of the comte de Chambord.*

### Chapitre XIII — Fin de l'Expédition du Mexique

*Unanimous press complaints against the prolongation of the intervention. The United States gives Napoleon III one year to evacuate Mexico. Arrival of Baron Saillard at Mexico bearing a letter from Drouyn de Lhuys; Maximilian refuses to receive him. Disastrous situation of the Mexican Empire; Maximilian's discouragement. Lack of understanding between Maximilian and Bazaine; Maximilian requests Bazaine's recall. Capture of Matamoros by the republicans. Almonte's useless mission to Napoleon III. Maximilian wants to abdicate; his wife prevents him and departs for Paris. Clerical plot discovered at Mexico. Republican troop advances. The United States insists on the departure of the French army. General Castelnau sent to Mexico. Maximilian and Father Fisher. Bazaine's extraordinary conduct. Fould's confidential report. Interview of Maximilian and Castelnau. Bazaine's double game revealed.*

### Chapitre XIV — Le Clergé en France (1863–1867)

*Polemic between Bishop Dupanloup and Edgar Quinet. The cult of the Virgin. Publication of the *Vie de Jésus*. Efforts of Lyonnais Catholics to preserve their liturgy. The Malines congress. The Encyclical and the Syllabus. Government prohibits publication of the first part of the Encyclical. Letter of the Bishop of Montpellier. Grief of the liberal Catholics; the Encyclical commented by the Bishop of Orléans. The Emperor authorises Donnet to convoke the provincial synod at Poitiers. Changes in the Protestant Church: universal suffrage applied to consistory elections. Liberal Protestants and Orthodox Protestants. Foundation of the Union protestante libérale. Struggle between the Presbyteral Council and Pastor Martin Paschoud. Elections of 1865: Guizot elected only on the second ballot with a weak majority. The liberal Protestants in vain demand a second Presbyteral Council. The minister of cults takes sides against the liberal Protestants.*

## Notes

- **Series:** *Histoire illustrée du second empire* by Taxile Delord; this is the **Tome quatrième** of the illustrated edition (Baillière [1880–83]), corresponding to the fourth volume of the original 1869–75 Baillière set (OCLC 6266334). Delord's **[Tome V](delord-histoire-second-empire-t5.md)** (1874; 1867–1869) continues the series in this wiki. **[Tome III](../../Rest%20of%20Europe%201800-1865/wiki/sources/delord-histoire-second-empire-t3.md)** (1857–1864) and **[Tome II](../../Rest%20of%20Europe%201800-1865/wiki/sources/delord-histoire-second-empire-t2.md)** sit in the 1800–1865 wiki.
- **Placement:** Rest of Europe **1866–1919** wiki — the narrative's centre of gravity is the **1866** Austro-Prussian War (Sadowa, Custozza, Prague) and its aftermath (Venetian cession, Luxembourg question, Mexican evacuation). The volume also covers the 1864–1865 sessions and continues into religious politics down to 1867, but the decisive watershed of Sadowa anchors it in this wiki.
- **Voice:** Contemporary republican narrative history; represent what Delord states, without modern scholarly reframing.
- **Illustrations:** 59 wood engravings in the text (not preserved in this raw OCR).
- **Related in corpus:** Sybel *Die Begründung des deutschen Reiches* (vols III–IV covering the same Austro-Prussian crisis from a Prussian-nationalist perspective) sits under the German Unification shelf in this wiki. Delord's French republican viewpoint complements the German sources.
