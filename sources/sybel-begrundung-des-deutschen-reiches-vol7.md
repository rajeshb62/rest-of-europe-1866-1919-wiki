## Chapters

### Dreiundzwanzigstes Buch: Deutschland und Frankreich (Germany and France)

- **1. Kapitel: Das erste Zollparlament (First Customs Parliament, Reichstag legislation)** — The first Zollparlament (Customs Parliament) convened in 1868, bringing together deputies from the North German Confederation and the southern German states; the legislative work of the North German Reichstag, including military laws, fiscal legislation, and the gradual harmonisation of legal and economic frameworks.
- **2. Kapitel: Schwankungen der französischen Politik (Fluctuations in French policy, Ollivier, Napoleon's reforms)** — The internal political evolution of the Second French Empire: the liberal reforms of Émile Ollivier, the shift toward a 'Liberal Empire', the weaknesses of Napoleon III's foreign policy, French public opinion, and the growing tension between Paris and Berlin.
- **3. Kapitel: Die luxemburgische Frage (The Luxembourg question)** — The Luxembourg Crisis of 1867: Napoleon III's bid to purchase Luxembourg from the Netherlands, Bismarck's initial encouragement and subsequent denunciation, the threat of war, and the London Conference (May 1867) that guaranteed Luxembourg's neutrality and led to the dismantling of its fortress.
- **4. Kapitel: Die Militärkonventionen (Military conventions with southern states)** — The secret military conventions between Prussia and the southern German states (Baden, Bavaria, Württemberg, Hesse-Darmstadt) placing their armies under Prussian command in wartime — a crucial step toward military unity that would prove decisive in 1870.

### Vierundzwanzigstes Buch: Ursprung des französischen Kriegs (Origin of the French War)

The immediate origins of the Franco-Prussian War: the offer of the Spanish crown to Prince Leopold of Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen (1870), the diplomatic crisis that followed, Bismarck's role in promoting and then managing the candidature, the Ems Telegram (the 'Ems Dispatch'), its deliberate editing by Bismarck to provoke French outrage, the French declaration of war (19 July 1870), and the mobilisation of the North German Confederation and the southern German states in a unified war effort against France.
