Zhevakhov — Memoirs of a Deputy Ober-Procurator of the Holy Synod, Vol. 1

Prince Nikolai Davidovich Zhevakhov (1874–1946) — Воспоминанія. Товарища Оберъ-Прокурора Св. Синода. Первый томъ. Сентябрь 1915 г. — Мартъ 1917 г. Munich: R. Oldenbourg Press, 1923.

AuthorPrince N. D. Zhevakhov — official of the State Chancellery; from autumn 1916 deputy ober-procurator of the Holy Synod (under Ober-Procurator N. P. Raev)
EditionMunich, 1923 (emigration); pre-reform orthography
IA idzhevahovndvospominanijaoberprokuroras2 (oras2 ≠ “volume 2” — this is the first volume)
PeriodSeptember 1915 – March 1917 (to release from arrest by the Provisional Government)
StructureChapters 1–94 + Conclusion; ~26,410 OCR lines
Next volumeVol. 2 (1928) — March 1917 – January 1920

Contents. First-person testimony of a senior synodal official of the monarchy’s last year and a half: the Brotherhood of St. Joasaph of Belgorod and delivery of the Nechaian Icon to Stavka (Mogilev, October 1915); audiences with Nicholas II and Alexandra Feodorovna; Metropolitan Pitirim; resignation of A. N. Volzhin and Zhevakhov’s appointment as deputy ober-procurator (autumn 1916); synodal commissions and the Duma (speeches of Miliukov, Lvov); an extended sketch of Rasputin and “God-seeking”; journey to the Caucasus (January–February 1917); return to Petrograd in the February days; memorable sitting of the Holy Synod of 26 February; arrest of 28 February, confinement in the Ministerial Pavilion, news of the abdication, release by Kerensky.

Character of the source. Monarchical, church-mystical émigré memoir; the author defends the line of the Sovereign and the Empress and explains the collapse through a “fateful epoch” and the struggle against “tsarism.” Not an operational-staff monument (cf. Denikin, Essays on the Russian Troubles), but a view from the Synod and Tsarskoe Selo. Chronologically joins the end of Witte, Vols. 2–3 (1960) (Witte breaks off ~1911) and the beginning of Denikin’s volume (February–September 1917).

OCR. Medium: Roman chapter numerals often distorted (ГЛАВА Г. / ИП. / УГ.); Ѣ/Е, І/И; «Тоасафъ» = Joasaph; years sometimes “1945/46/47” for 1915–17. Headings below reconstructed from the table of contents and repeated forms in the body; raw stream is in the raw file.

I. Path to Stavka and the court (chs. 1–23) — autumn 1915 – early 1916

Chapter 1. General meeting of the Brotherhood of St. Joasaph, 4 September 1915. Report of Colonel O.

Meeting of the Brotherhood of Saint Joasaph of Belgorod on the anniversary of the glorification; chairman Gen. L. K. Artamonov; vice-chairmen — Archpriest A. G. Malyarevsky and Zhevakhov. Author’s absence from the meeting; on the evening of 5 September Malyarevsky recounts a report that became a “turning point” — the seed of the mission with the icon.

Chapter 2. Mistress of the Robes E. A. Naryshkina

Chapter 3. Count Ya. N. Rostovtsov

Court intermediaries; Rostovtsov as a channel to the Empress.

Chapter 4. Meeting with Ober-Procurator of the Holy Synod A. N. Volzhin. Farewell conversation with Archpriest A. G. Malyarevsky. Departure from Petrograd

Malyarevsky insists on a visit to Volzhin before removing the icons; formal mission and telegrams to the Most Reverend of Kharkov and Kursk.

Chapter 6. On the way to Stavka. Conversation with priest Aleksandr Yakovlev

A rural priest-companion; piety of “seeing the Tsar Batiushka.”

Headquarters of the Supreme Commander; procession of the cross; name-day of the Heir (5 October) as the calendar frame of the icon’s arrival.

Corruption of the “mechanism” of power “at the top”; ministers “in captivity” to the Duma and the press; contrast with the still-holding lower police. Pitirim as a church-political figure of the capital.

Summer 1916 in the countryside without newspapers; at Liuban station — news of Volzhin’s resignation and of his own appointment. Raev — new ober-procurator; Zhevakhov — deputy ober-procurator.

The author’s summing-up of the Volzhin history.

Two weeks’ absence from Petersburg; reaction of the newspapers.

Completeness of the set

LayerStatus
A. Continuous block “Synod / court to the collapse”Yes — September 1915 → March 1917, chs. 1–94, closed by this volume
B. Full authorial cycle of the MemoirsNot in one volume — there is Vol. 2 (March 1917 – January 1920: Kiev, the hetman, Volunteer orbit, emigration, the Church after the revolution). In the preface to Vol. 2 the author mentions a possible Vol. III (European literature on the “Jewish question,” etc.) — not extracted, pending an IA id
